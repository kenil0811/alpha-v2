# Midpoint review and charter revision

Run this when roughly half the time on a project has passed. It turns the charter (SKILL.md, step 4) into a living document. Built from standard midpoint reflection, team learning and charter revision practice, in our own layout.

## 1. Each member, alone first (about 30 minutes)
**Score the team on the three effectiveness tests, 1 (very poor) to 10 (excellent), with evidence for each:**
| Test | Score | Evidence (results, client feedback, deadlines hit or missed, incidents) |
|---|---|---|
| Delivers what stakeholders expect | | |
| Members and the team are growing | | |
| People would choose to work together again | | |

**Assess four process areas, with one example each of a high and a low point:**
- Workload: is work spread fairly, and is each person's expertise actually used?
- Communication and coordination: does information reach the people who need it in time?
- Time: how were deadlines and competing demands handled? Where was time lost?
- Meetings: in person, video and asynchronous: which were worth the time? What would make them shorter or better?

**Assess yourself:** the two or three ways you add unique value; where you could grow (including stepping back from roles you dominate so others can learn); one thing you will do differently.

## 2. Look at the evidence per piece of work
For each major deliverable so far: the outcome (result, feedback, other indicators); the process losses (where effort was wasted, duplicated or uncoordinated); what the team learned. Name two things to do differently on the next piece.

## 3. Team session (60 to 90 minutes)
Share individual scores first, then discuss gaps between members' scores. Agree:
1. **Two or three strengths** and why each helps the team perform.
2. **The three biggest weaknesses or friction points** (tension, frustration, inefficiency), tied to one of the three effectiveness tests.
3. **Three actions**, each behavioural and specific ("the editor sends the draft 48 hours before the client deadline", not "communicate better"), each linked to a weakness it fixes.

Test for coherence: every action should trace back to a weakness or strength named above.

## 4. Peer feedback (optional, 360)
Design a short survey every member completes about every other member: a few ratings (reliability, quality, collaboration, communication) plus two open questions (what this person should keep doing; one thing they could change). Report results anonymised and aggregated. Pair with start/stop/continue (see `team-conflict-trust-and-feedback`).

## 5. Revise the charter
Change the charter wherever an agreed action touches it (roles, SLA, meeting rhythm, sign-off rules, task plan). Show the changes visibly (marked "revised at midpoint") so everyone sees what moved. If an outside sponsor or manager reviewed the midpoint, address their comments in the same revision.

## Signs the review was shallow
- A play-by-play of events with no diagnosis.
- Actions that are slogans rather than behaviours.
- No change to the charter afterwards.
