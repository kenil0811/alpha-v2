# Fast-forming and distributed teams

Use when a team forms quickly, changes membership often, has to learn a new way of working, or is spread across sites and time zones. Synthesised from published work on teaming, team learning and global teams; wording and layout ours.

## 1. Teams that form on the fly
Many teams are temporary mixes of people who have not worked together. Treat it as two jobs:

**Hardware (project management)**
- **Scope** the challenge: what must be achieved, which expertise is needed, which parts are uncertain.
- **Structure lightly** ("scaffolding"): a one-page who's-who with each member's background and expertise; one shared channel; a short launch briefing that gives each subgroup a role and direction; temporary co-location or site visits for high-priority work.
- **Sort the tasks**: not everything needs collaboration. Split work into pieces that can be done alone, pieces that need hand-offs, and the few that need everyone at once; only convene for the last kind.

**Software (team leadership)**
- **Psychological safety**: the leader models asking questions, admits what they don't know, and invites disagreement regardless of rank.
- **Treat failure as information**: small experiments, quick review of what failed, no blame for intelligent misses.
- **Use conflict**: ask each side to explain how they reached their view and which values sit behind it; balance advocacy with real inquiry ("Is this the only way to see it? What might I be missing?").
- Behaviours to reinforce: speaking up, experimenting, reflecting on a regular rhythm (daily or weekly), listening intently, integrating different views into new options.

## 2. Teams learning a new method, tool or process
The fastest-learning teams in published studies shared five habits:
1. **Choose members deliberately** for how well they work together, not only for availability or seniority; keep the early group stable.
2. **Frame it as a team learning challenge**, not a plug-in tool: say why it matters and that everyone's routine will change.
3. **Make it safe to speak up**: the leader says explicitly that anyone who sees a problem is expected to raise it, and acknowledges their own fallibility.
4. **Prepare together**: a dry run or rehearsal before the first live use; the leader takes part.
5. **Debrief early uses**: after the first few runs, review what happened and adjust before habits set.

A high-status expert leader is not enough; how the leader manages the team's learning matters more.

## 3. Distributed teams: the SPLIT check
Social distance (weak emotional and cognitive connection) is the core risk. Check five sources:
| Source | What to look for | Moves |
|---|---|---|
| **Structure** (who sits where) | Unequal subgroups by site; lone members ("isolates"); members near the leader or head office getting more voice | Rotate meeting times; give each site visible roles; leader checks in with isolates directly; counter status gaps by naming each person's expertise |
| **Process** | Mistrust from timing habits (for example a site that answers email once a day) | Build *direct knowledge* (learn about each other's context; the first minutes of calls for non-work talk) and *reflected knowledge* (see your own habits as the other site sees them, ideally by visiting); encourage task disagreement on purpose; name helpful differences |
| **Language** | Fluent speakers dominating; less fluent members going quiet | See SKILL.md step 6 |
| **Identity** | People identifying with their site or function first | Keep a shared goal in front of the team; mark team milestones together; treat culture as more than nationality (profession, region, generation) |
| **Technology** | Over-reliance on email; one medium for everything | Use live channels (call, video, chat) to persuade or build buy-in; delayed channels (email, shared docs) to record and follow up; send important messages both ways ("redundant communication"); richer media help distant teams but can inflame a team already in a long-running dispute |

Leaders set the medium by example: if they want stronger ties, they call rather than email.
