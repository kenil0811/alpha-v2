# Diagnosing and shaping culture

Use before a culture change, a merger or acquisition, a rapid growth phase, or when a team's way of working clashes with its strategy. Built on the competing values culture assessment and published work on leading by leveraging culture; wording ours.

## 1. The four culture types (competing values)
Two tensions: flexibility vs stability and control; internal focus vs external focus.
| Type | Focus | Leaders | Success means | Taken too far |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Collaborate** (flexible, internal) | People, commitment, teamwork | Mentor, facilitate | Developed people, engagement | Permissive, results neglected |
| **Create** (flexible, external) | Innovation, risk, growth | Entrepreneur, visionary | Newest or unique offer | Chaos, one more idea, nothing predictable |
| **Compete** (stable, external) | Results, speed, market share | Hard-driving, demanding | Beating competitors | Self-interest, conflict, people burned |
| **Control** (stable, internal) | Process, efficiency, reliability | Coordinator, organiser | Dependable, low-cost delivery | Bureaucracy, stagnation |

No type is "right"; moving toward one means moving away from its opposite (collaborate vs compete, create vs control).

## 2. Run the assessment
1. Each person answers six items, dividing 100 points across four statements (one per type) for **now** and again for **what we must become** to succeed:
   - dominant characteristics (family-like / entrepreneurial / results-driven / structured);
   - leadership style (nurturing / innovating / no-nonsense / smooth-running);
   - management of people (consensus / individual freedom / high demands / security and predictability);
   - organisational glue (loyalty / innovation / winning / rules);
   - strategic emphasis (human development / new opportunities / stretch targets / stability);
   - criteria of success (people / product leadership / market wins / efficiency).
2. Average each type's points across the six items, separately for now and future.
3. Plot both profiles on the four-quadrant grid (now solid, future dotted).
4. Score by statement content: first statement in each item = collaborate, second = create, third = compete, fourth = control.
5. Compare: your view vs others', unit vs unit, now vs future. A gap of about 10 points or more on a type is worth discussing (rule of thumb).

## 3. Turn the gaps into a plan (eight steps)
1. **Change**: which types must increase and which decrease?
2. **Meaning**: for each type, what the change does and does not mean (for example "more compete" means clear targets, not internal rivalry).
3. **Stories**: one or two real incidents that show the culture you want.
4. **Strategies**: two or three, each split into do more of, stop doing, start doing.
5. **Small wins**: steps you can take this month to build momentum.
6. **Measures and milestones**: indicators, how they are measured, by when.
7. **Leadership**: what leaders must learn or model.
8. **Communication**: the symbols, rituals and channels that will carry the new culture.

## 4. Make culture a working management tool
Culture steers behaviour through shared norms (what people expect of each other) more than rules or pay. It helps performance only if it is:
- **Strategically relevant**: the norms point at what the strategy needs (service, speed, safety, innovation).
- **Strong**: high *agreement* on what matters and high *intensity* (people care and will call out lapses). Agreement without intensity is empty; intensity without agreement splits the firm.
- **Adaptive**: it includes norms that welcome change and sensible risk, so it does not harden around yesterday's strategy.

Three levers:
1. **Selection**: hire for culture fit as well as job fit; be explicit in job adverts and interviews about what it is like to work here so candidates self-select.
2. **Socialisation and training**: structured onboarding, early assignments that teach the norms, mentors, shared stories.
3. **Rewards**: pay and promotion for the behaviours wanted, plus social recognition in front of peers, which often works faster than money.

Watch for: hiring for "fit" that becomes sameness (pair with the diversity steps in `team-launch-and-charter`).
