# Debiasing toolkit for important decisions

Practical checks against narrow thinking about the future, the objectives and the options. Synthesised from published work on outsmarting your own biases; wording and layout ours. Use alongside SKILL.md's decision architect steps.

## When to slow down
Fast intuition is fine for routine choices. Switch to these tools when a decision is one-off, costly to reverse, or made by tired or emotionally invested people.

## Future: stop betting on one forecast
- **Three estimates**: for any key number (demand, price, cost, time), write low, expected and high, each separately; the range is usually wider than people first think.
- **Average independent forecasts**: collect estimates from several people before they talk, then average.
- **Premortem**: imagine it is a year later and the decision failed; each person writes why. Fix the most plausible causes now.
- **Outside view**: ask how similar projects usually turn out (the base rate), and what you would advise a friend facing those odds. Adjust your inside estimate toward it.

## Objectives: widen before you narrow
- Each person lists objectives **alone first**, then pool them into a master list; people typically recognise many relevant goals they did not write down.
- **Cycle through objectives one at a time** (for each option, ask how well it serves cost, then quality, then risk...), rather than judging everything at once.
- Rank the pooled list before looking at options.

## Options: never decide "yes or no" on one
- **Joint evaluation**: compare at least two or three real alternatives side by side; options judged alone get judged on irrelevant cues.
- **Vanishing options test**: "If we could not choose any option on the table, what would we do?" This forces new alternatives.
- Look for a combination or a staged version (test small before committing).

## Structure that removes noise
- **Blind review**: hide names or irrelevant attributes when scoring (applications, bids, proposals).
- **Checklists and structured interviews**: the same criteria and questions for every case, scored the same way.
- **Simple scoring rules**: weights set in advance; combine with judgment, since rules carry their builders' biases.

## Guard against motivated bias (emotional attachment)
- **Trip wires**: set in advance the point at which you stop or re-decide (a spend cap, a deadline, a minimum result).
- **Decision points**: softer trip wires that trigger a pause and review, not an automatic action; useful in bidding or negotiations where escalation is the risk.
- Separate the people who championed a project from the review of whether to continue it.
