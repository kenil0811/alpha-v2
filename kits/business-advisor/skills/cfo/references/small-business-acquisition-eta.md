# Acquiring a small business (search funds and ETA)

> **Area:** `small-business-acquisition-eta` · owned by the CFO skill. **Use when:** Buy and run a company of roughly $1.5M to $5M EBITDA with investors behind you (traditional search fund, independent sponsor, family office, long-hold vehicle) - pick the vehicle, set searcher economics and criteria, win the seller, value and structure the deal, and hand over from the founder. Use when choosing among buyout vehicles or raising investor money for a search; for a self-funded SBA purchase use buying-a-small-business.

**Outcome:** a searcher (or small buyer) with clear criteria, aligned investors, a financeable deal with a motivated seller, and a planned transition to running the company.

## When to use

- "I want to buy and run a company rather than start one."
- "What makes a good search-fund target?"
- "How do searcher economics work?"
- "Fund, fundless sponsor, family office or long-hold: which vehicle fits?"
- Planning the seller's handover.

## Method

### 1. Pick the vehicle
- **Traditional search fund:** investors pay the searcher's salary and costs for about 2 years ("search capital"), then get the right to fund the deal. Stanford studies: about 32.6% IRR and 5.5x ROI (2020 study), 35.1% and 4.5x (2024 study), across all core funds including failed searches; wide dispersion, and about a third of searches never buy. Underwrite against the figures excluding the top performers (about 33% and 3.2x in 2024).
- **Self-funded search / independent (fundless) sponsor:** find the deal first, then raise equity deal by deal. More control, harder financing; expect lenders to scrutinise you more; NDAs may carry non-circumvention clauses.
- **Single or multi-family office:** invests one or a few families' money, often directly, with patient capital.
- **Long-hold holding company or fund:** holds 10 to 15+ years; compounding, lower transaction costs and deferred tax can beat a 3 to 5 year flip on after-tax multiple.
Before starting, partners should agree on goals, money, hours, roles and non-starter industries or places.

### 2. Searcher economics (typical)
- Searcher earns 15% to 30% of common equity in three equal tranches: at acquisition; over 4 to 5 years of service; and on performance (IRR hurdles, often vesting straight-line from 20% to 35% net investor IRR). ROI tests are often preferred for holds over 5 years.
- Investors get a preference (capital back, often with a small return) before the searcher's equity pays.
- Search capital usually converts into the deal securities with a step-up (for example 50%).
- Investors should bring advice, credibility and introductions, not only money.

### 3. Set criteria (aim: a good company in a great industry)
| Desirable | Undesirable |
|---|---|
| Fragmented, growing, sizeable industry with many firms in your size band | Consolidated or declining industry; fierce competition, low barriers; strong customer pricing power |
| Simple operations, early in life cycle, long product life | Unpredictable outside forces: regulation, payer risk, fast tech change, unhedgeable commodities, unions, fads, high cyclicality |
| Healthy margins (return on tangible capital above about 20%) | Turnaround |
| Competitive advantage; recurring revenue; history of cash flow | Customer over about 30% of sales; high churn |
| Seller motivated by non-business reasons (retirement, health) | Under about $10M revenue or $1.5M EBITDA; no management bench |
| $10M to $30M revenue, $1.5M+ EBITDA (success most common at $1.5M to $5M) | Competitive auction; public-to-private deals |
| Many growth paths; solid middle managers; financing available; fair price; exit possible in 3 to 6 years | |

Criteria cut three risks: not finding a company, not closing the deal, and not running it well. No target meets all; know which trade-offs you accept, and know "when to take the train". Many top-performing search deals broke at least one criterion.

### 4. Find and win the seller
- Industry-focused searches build expertise and owner credibility; generalist searches cast a wider net. Retired CEOs or trade-association heads make good advisors.
- Run the funnel: many owner conversations, perhaps 10 LOIs, diligence on 1 to 3 to close one.
- Use brokers (agree a standard fee) and direct outreach; expect scepticism about a first-time CEO.
- Be flexible on structure; sellers care about more than price (not relocating, keeping staff, keeping a small stake). One searcher won as third-highest bidder by meeting such wishes.
- Talk to lenders early so you do not chase an unfinanceable deal.

### 5. Evaluate in three passes
1. **First pass:** limited data; decide whether it deserves time.
2. **Valuation / LOI:** confirm the owner truly wants to sell; analyse industry and position; value with a buyout model (IRR and ROI under scenarios), public comps and precedent deals (with a size discount), DCF rarely, assets only for asset-heavy firms; draft major terms; list diligence priorities; brief investors; get lender feedback. The LOI confirms intent, sets value, triggers bigger spending and usually grants exclusivity.
3. **Comprehensive diligence** (see `buyout-due-diligence`). Separate the "ants" from the "elephants". Be efficient with the seller's limited time; a seller selling for the first time can sour fast. Step back regularly to see the cumulative effect of small changes; ask outsiders to sanity-check.
Price issues: working-capital shortfalls cut price dollar for dollar; earnings shortfalls cut it at the multiple; bridge with earn-out, seller debt or seller equity. Terms issues: specific indemnities, larger baskets and caps, longer survival, more reps, closing conditions.
Do not pay for annualised run-rate earnings; lenders lend on trailing results. Add-backs must be accepted by the lender too, or the gap becomes extra equity, and must be written into the EBITDA definitions in the loan, any earn-out and management targets.

### 6. Capital structure
Typical shape: senior debt about 1x EBITDA, seller financing 1x to 1.5x, the rest investor equity (often half or more). Investor equity may be participating preferred, or a mix of redeemable preferred (with a coupon) and participating preferred. Operating performance, not the preferred structure, drives most of the outcome. See `acquisition-debt-capacity` and `lbo-model-returns`.

### 7. Hand over from the founder
- Decide whether the seller leaves at close or stays for a defined transition (1 to 12 months) in a non-CEO role.
- A seller who stays can calm staff, customers and suppliers and teach the business. Risks: blurred authority, reluctance to let go, disputes over earn-outs.
- Keep control: the searcher must be able to end the arrangement. Paid consulting with a set number of hours and restrictive covenants (non-compete, non-solicit) works well.
- Value levers after close: operations (sales, pricing, new services, geography, add-ons, margins), finance (structure, lower capital intensity) and multiple (buy low, professionalise, grow, sell well).

## Worked pattern

A searcher buys a pest-control company: $12M revenue, $2.4M EBITDA, 85% recurring contracts, founder retiring.
- Price 5.0x = $12.0M; fees $0.5M.
- Senior debt 1.25x = $3.0M; seller note 1.25x = $3.0M (5 years, interest only for 2); investor equity $6.5M (search capital of $0.4M converts at a 50% step-up, counting as $0.6M of it).
- Searcher equity 25%: about 8.3% at close, 8.3% over 4 years, 8.3% vesting between 20% and 35% investor IRR.
- Base case: EBITDA grows about 9% a year (growth plus margin) to $4.1M; exit year 6 at 6.0x = $24.6M; debt left $1.5M; equity $23.1M. Investors hold 75% to 83% depending on vesting: about $17M to $19M on $6.5M, 2.7x to 2.9x, 18% to 19% IRR. Below 20%, so the performance tranche does not vest. Pessimistic case (flat, 4x exit) leaves the searcher little.
- Lesson: price and operations matter more than structure; push for a lower price or a bigger seller note before closing.

## Common mistakes

- Copying the standard criteria list without knowing why each item is there.
- Waiting for a perfect target and never buying; or buying a turnaround to end the search.
- Chasing auctions against strategics and funded PE.
- Agreeing add-backs the lender will not accept.
- Paying for run-rate earnings.
- Letting the founder stay without clear authority lines.
- Underestimating timelines: 4 to 12 months from intro to close is common.
