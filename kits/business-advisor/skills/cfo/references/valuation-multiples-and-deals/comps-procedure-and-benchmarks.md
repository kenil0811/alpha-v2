# Comparable-company procedure and sector benchmark ranges

## Procedure for a trading-comps valuation
1. Pull a screen of listed firms in the target's sector with: price, enterprise value, LTM and
   next-twelve-months (NTM) EBITDA, LTM and NTM revenue, LTM and NTM EPS, business description.
2. Add four helper columns: size (EV), profitability (NTM EBITDA / NTM revenue), EV / NTM
   EBITDA, price / NTM EPS.
3. Pick 6 to 10 comparables close on all three of size, profitability and business description.
   Drop firms with negative or tiny EBITDA or EPS (their multiples are meaningless).
4. Take the **median** of each multiple across the chosen comps.
5. Apply: price = median P/E × target NTM EPS. EV = median EV/EBITDA × target NTM EBITDA, then
   equity = EV − net debt − minority interest − preferred stock; divide by shares.
6. Show both estimates beside the DCF and dividend model values and the market price (a
   "football field"). A wide spread across methods is normal; explain it rather than average it away.

## Sector ranges, US listed companies, autumn 2025 (NTM basis)
Summarised from a comparables screen (interquartile range / median). Use only as a
sanity check on a chosen multiple; they move with markets and are for listed, mostly larger firms.

| Sector | EV / NTM EBITDA | Price / NTM EPS | EV / NTM revenue |
|---|---|---|---|
| Technology | 9–25 (15) | 15–38 (24) | 1.9–6.8 (3.9) |
| Health care | 9–22 (12) | 13–31 (19) | 2.0–9.2 (3.9) |
| Industrials | 8–16 (12) | 14–26 (19) | 1.2–3.9 (2.1) |
| Consumer discretionary | 8–14 (10) | 11–24 (15) | 0.8–2.4 (1.4) |
| Consumer staples | 7.5–13 (10) | 10–21 (15) | 0.6–2.4 (1.3) |
| Communication and media | 6–14 (8) | 8–26 (16) | 0.8–3.1 (1.7) |
| Materials | 6–11 (8) | 11–19 (14) | 0.9–2.7 (1.6) |
| Energy | 4–8 (6) | 10–19 (14) | 1.1–3.3 (2.0) |
| Utilities | 10–13 (11.5) | 17–22 (19.5) | 3.4–5.8 (4.4) |
| Financials | 7–16 (10) | 9.5–23 (14) | 1.5–5.0 (2.9) |
| Real estate | 12–17 (15) | 23–59 (35) | 5–11 (9) |

Sector ETF data (late 2025) showed forward P/E from about 17 (energy,
financials) to over 30 (technology, real estate), and sector betas from about 0.6 (staples,
utilities, health care) to about 1.3 (consumer discretionary, materials).

Small private firms usually trade well below listed medians because of size, concentration and
illiquidity; deals for owner-run firms often sit in low-to-mid single digits of EBITDA
(standard practice). P/E for real estate and financials is a poor guide;
use sector-specific measures there.
