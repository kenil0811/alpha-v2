# Buy-and-build: the math, the engine and integration

## 1. Where the value comes from (standard practice decomposition)
Platform value at exit minus everything paid splits into four parts:

| Part | Formula |
|---|---|
| Multiple arbitrage | add-on EBITDA x (platform multiple - add-on multiple paid) |
| Synergies | synergy EBITDA x platform multiple, less one-off integration cost |
| Scale re-rating | (exit multiple - platform entry multiple) x combined EBITDA at exit |
| Organic growth | growth in EBITDA x exit multiple (as in any deal) |

Own numbers: platform $5M EBITDA bought at 8x ($40M); three add-ons with $1M EBITDA each at 5x ($15M);
$0.5M of synergies; integration cost $1.5M. Combined EBITDA $8.5M (no organic growth) sells at 9x = $76.5M.
- Arbitrage 3 x (8 - 5) = $9.0M
- Synergies 0.5 x 8 = $4.0M
- Scale re-rating (9 - 8) x 8.5 = $8.5M
- Total $21.5M = $76.5M - $55M paid; less $1.5M integration = $20M net.
Test each part: arbitrage disappears if competition pushes add-on prices up to platform levels; synergies
need named owners and dates; re-rating depends on the exit market. A plan that works only with re-rating
is a bet on the market, not on the work.

Funding: add-ons are usually paid from a committed delayed-draw or acquisition facility plus new equity;
earn-outs bridge price gaps with small sellers. Keep pro forma leverage inside covenants after each deal
(see `acquisition-debt-capacity`).

Market figures: platforms are reported to trade at about a 20%+ premium to add-ons; follow-on deals are over
70% of PE deals; a $10M to $25M company traded around 6x in 2019 vs 10x+ for $250M+ deals.

## 2. Build an acquisition engine (a veterinary roll-up, 2022 to 2024 practitioner decks)
- A dedicated business-development team with industry experience.
- A referral agreement with a large industry vendor gave proprietary access (five-year term, renewable);
  it converted to LOIs better than any other source.
- Track leads, LOIs, closings and pipeline by source; proprietary or internal leads were a minority of
  leads but a much larger share of LOIs and closings than broker leads.
- Pace: about one acquisition a week at peak, with revenue up about 200% and EBITDA about 500% in a year.
- Keep a live add-on pipeline table: target, region, product set, approximate revenue, status (IOI, LOI,
  runner-up, on hold), strategic rationale. Put deals on hold when regulation clouds their category.

## 3. Run the combined company
- Span of control in a multi-site roll-up (same case): one COO over all sites; regional directors over
  about 20 to 30 sites; district managers over about 5; a manager per site.
- Shared tools: a site dashboard and productivity score, labour scheduling, central purchasing.
- Organic engine per site: price soon after each acquisition (about 30 days) and regularly after;
  recruiting team; capacity-based marketing; renovate or relocate small sites; new revenue lines.
- People as a lever where staff are the asset: a living wage plus revenue share lifted staff retention
  (about 77% industry vs 92% in the case) and job applicants by about half.

## 4. Integration checklist (a digital agency roll-up, 2026; one 120-person firm integrated in about 6 months)
- **People:** plan founders' new roles or exits; retain key managers and staff; pick one operating model.
- **Clients:** protect retention; cross-sell each firm's services to the other's clients; align pricing and billing.
- **Technology and administration:** one ERP and one tech stack (14 platforms merged in the case); adopt
  the better firm's proprietary tools.
- **Synergies:** list revenue and cost synergies with values (for example clearing an acquired backlog
  unlocked over $1M of contracted recurring revenue; removing duplicate spend and card fees).
- **Platform evolution:** compare acquisition profile vs today on positioning, sector expertise,
  technology, management depth, headcount, client count and largest-client share (the case went from
  75 to 300+ staff and about 400 to 1,200 clients while the top client stayed at about 2%).

## 5. Choosing growth levers in a lower-middle-market platform (2026 practitioner view)
Score each holding on: invest in sales and marketing; open alternative channels (distribution, big-box,
rental, direct online); new products and new geographies; web and data tools; professionalise
management; add-ons. In a manufacturing case, adding three engineers to a two-person team drove a run of
new products, and new channels carried growth. Name the thesis risk at entry (there, electric vehicles
shrinking a fuel source) and its mitigants (diversify the product line).
