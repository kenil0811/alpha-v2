# Pitching investors, and structuring a small raise

From VC practice (2019) and an entrepreneurship case on a small food venture.

## Running the pitch process
- Have at least ten people review the deck before any investor sees it.
- A warm referral makes a meeting several times likelier than a cold approach; most deals come from networks
  (see `cfo/references/vc-deal-screening/vc-decision-benchmarks.md`).
- Pitch less important prospects first, to practise; save the best-fit investors for when the story is tight.
- Debrief after every meeting: what was asked, what fell flat; fix the deck before the next one.
- Act on feedback. When an investor names a milestone ("come back when you have X"), treat it as a test:
  hit it and go back.
- Calibrate expectations: most investors pass, and a pass is often about fit, not quality.
- Deck order: problem, solution, why now, market, competition, product, business model, team, financials,
  the ask (the common investor template). Investors spend a few minutes per deck, longest on team,
  financials and the ask (standard practice).

## Why ventures fail
No market need is the most cited cause, then running out of money and the wrong team. In health and
deep-tech cases: a proof of concept that doesn't actually prove the concept; human results far harder than
animal results; hospital sales cycles and the cost to reach breakeven both underestimated. Plan the raise to
cover these.

## Small raises from friends, family and local investors
- **Units:** sell investment units that mix equity with debt (debentures). The debt part pays back first and
  can carry interest; the equity part gives the upside.
- **Staged capital calls:** investors commit a total but pay in stages; a holder who misses a call forfeits
  some or all of what was paid. This keeps commitments real without asking for all the cash up front.
- **What investors want over time:** early on, repayment and some control; later, capital gains with less
  control. Design the units so the debt is repaid first and control steps back as it is.
- **Key partners:** offer equity to a partner the business depends on (a supplier, a distributor) so its
  incentives line up with yours.
- **Legal form:** choose the entity (corporation, LLC, partnership) for liability, tax and how easily new
  investors can come in, before taking the money.
