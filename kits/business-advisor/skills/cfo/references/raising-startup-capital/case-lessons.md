# Generalised case lessons on raising capital

From examples on financing new ventures, bootstrap finance, beating the odds and the start-up
process. Stated as general lessons in our own words.

| Situation | What happened | Lesson |
|---|---|---|
| A location-data venture needing a nationwide map of wireless signals | Rivals assumed it needed $100M+; it built cheap car-mounted kit and hired hourly drivers, staying under about a sixth of that | Redesign the build to fit the budget of the funders who will take the risk; avoid the valley of death |
| A power plant financed by international banks | The anchor customer wanted to swap in its own inexperienced builder; the banks balked until the original contractor stayed | Any change that raises perceived risk can unwind financing already agreed |
| A drug developer offered a quick, profitable supplement line | Its venture investors opposed it as a distraction from the blockbuster | Early investors' return model shapes later strategy; know it before taking their money |
| An e-paper maker funded by funds, a publisher and an equipment maker | Their goals split once progress was slower than hoped | Mixed investor types agree in good times and fight in bad |
| Two engineers rejected by VCs for a year | Put in about $12,500 each; years later, profitable, they were offered unsolicited VC money | VC rejection is often about fit, not quality; bootstrapping can work |
| A founder who wanted a bank loan | Kept clean books and a relationship with the branch for three years, then let the banker propose the loan | Court lenders before you need them |
| A parts business | Asked suppliers for 90 to 120 days on a first order; most agreed; it stayed cash-positive while growing | Supplier terms are early financing |
| A big corporate venture funded all at once | Money and status pushed it to defend the first plan | Fund in stages; too much capital too soon breeds confirmation bias |
| A distressed venture refinanced | New money demanded old money stay in and step back in priority | Keep a reserve; raising in distress is confiscatory |
| A fund's 468 investments over 16 years | About 60% of dollars returned less than cost; about 8% of dollars produced over 70% of returns | Venture portfolios are driven by a few winners; pitch accordingly |
