# First 100 days and the board

For a new owner-CEO taking over from a founder. Drawn from the Stanford search fund primer, Part VII (2021 edition), in our own words.

## Plan before close

- Write the plan during diligence: hour by hour for week one, daily for the next few weeks, weekly after that.
- Four workstreams: communication, education, evaluation, governance.
- Keep a private "strawman" strategy and operating plan as hypotheses to test, not to announce. Watch for confirmation bias.
- If the seller stays, sign a transition services agreement first: defined role (not CEO), hours, pay, end date, and the buyer's right to end it. Keep it short.

## Communication

**Order:** executive team → middle managers → all employees → key customers (by size, profit, influence) → other customers → suppliers and partners → industry and local press → investors (update within 30 days of close).

**Rules**
- Script it before close; one consistent message, assume every audience hears what the others hear.
- The seller may introduce you, but you run the meeting and own the message.
- No promises you may not keep. If no cuts are planned, say so; if cuts are coming, be honest and specific.
- Employees want: is my job safe, what changes, who is the new boss, what are the values. They do not need deal details or a grand vision.
- Formats: small company, one all-hands; larger or multi-site, senior team first, then town halls in week one; back it with a written note.
- Customers: research each key account first (contracts, order and payment history, service issues), brief the employee who owns the relationship, then call or visit together. A quick hello now and a deeper meeting a few weeks later buys learning time.
- Suppliers: a letter is usually enough; conversations with the main ones are a cheap source of insight.
- Pre-close investor update: debt amount and terms, final equity need, major diligence findings, key purchase-agreement terms.

## Education and evaluation

- Treat the first 100 days (often 6 to 12 months) as a second diligence with full access. Shift from investor to operator.
- Listen widely: one-to-ones, off-sites, town halls, surveys, a suggestion box. Visit every site within two weeks and again within 100 days.
- Open questions to ask everyone: what do you like here; what are you afraid I will do; what would you change; what must not change; how is the market shifting; what do you expect of me; how did your job's processes come about.
- Do the work: ride on sales calls, answer phones, work the floor, post orders.
- Master cash: how much, who controls it, how it is earned, billed and collected, why it swings. Many new CEOs sign every cheque and review daily receipts for a while.
- Build a daily dashboard: cash (collections, payables, balance), sales by line or region, pipeline, shipments and on-time delivery, key operating and service metrics. Manual at first if needed.
- Make no big changes early, except for illegal, unethical or dangerous practices, and blatantly disruptive people. New CEOs most often regret waiting too long on the latter.
- Do not listen to complaints about the predecessor; outwork everyone.

## Governance

- **Board size:** five or seven seats is most common: the CEO (two if partnered), investor directors, at least one independent if possible; the seller only if keeping a meaningful stake. Avoid customers and suppliers as directors. Fixed terms per seat make it easy to refresh.
- **Mix:** operator, industry or business-model expert, finance expert; fill the CEO's weakest areas; one or two directors willing to mentor between meetings.
- **Agree up front:** in person or remote; frequency; length; how early papers arrive; committees (audit, compensation); pay (at least expenses; often a modest annual and per-meeting fee); travel policy; D&O insurance.
- **Cadence:** first meeting 2 to 4 weeks after close; 2 to 3 meetings in the first 100 days; about six in year one; quarterly after that; extra for add-ons, refinancing or reorganisations. Lenders may get observer seats without votes.
- **Board papers** at least 3 days (ideally a week) ahead: highlights and lowlights, decisions needed with options, clear financials and a few key metrics, minutes and resolutions. Spend meetings on debate, not show-and-tell. End each with a short executive session without management.
- **First board pack:** company overview; deal recap (diligence findings, sources and uses, key terms, earn-outs, working-capital true-up, costs); management and seller role; what has happened since close; early concerns with options; recent results vs history, budget and the deal model; next-90-day plan with milestones; early opportunities (not a full strategy).
- **Good board behaviour:** candour and no surprises, bad news fast; board engages with strategy but does not run the company; knows how the business makes money; ties CEO pay to results; protects financial flexibility; maps key risks; CEO shares power openly.

## Ten traps for new CEOs (from a widely cited executive-onboarding list the primer reproduces)

Over-promising; rash moves or analysis paralysis; acting the know-it-all; relying on what worked elsewhere or the predecessor's budget; isolation; stifling dissent; trying to do it all alone; misreading where power really sits; picking the wrong battles; blaming the predecessor.
