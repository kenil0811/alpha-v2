# Search fund benchmarks (with study year)

Summarised from the Stanford GSB search fund studies of 2020 (data to end-2019, 401 funds) and 2024 (data to end-2023, 681 funds). Both cover only "core" search funds: a first-time searcher, backed by a group of investors, in the US and Canada. Self-funded, single-investor and long-term-hold searches are excluded. Use these as orders of magnitude and refresh them from the newest study before quoting them to anyone.

## Headline returns to original investors (pre-tax, search plus acquisition capital)

| Measure | 2020 study | 2024 study |
|---|---|---|
| Aggregate IRR, all funds incl. failed searches | 32.6% | 35.1% |
| Aggregate ROI (MOIC) | 5.5x | 4.5x |
| IRR excluding top 5 funds | 28.5% | 33.0% |
| ROI excluding top 5 funds | 2.9x | 3.2x |
| IRR of exited companies only | 35.4% | 42.9% |

Reading the numbers:
- A few big winners carry the averages; strip the top 3 to 5 and ROI roughly halves. Underwrite a single deal against the "excluding top 5" figures, not the headline.
- Aggregate IRR has sat in the low-to-mid 30s in every study since about 2001, while ROI and hold periods swing a lot.
- No statistically significant link was found between returns and searcher or company traits (2024). Recurring revenue share and expected industry growth also showed little correlation with returns (2020).

## Funnel and outcomes

- Searches that end without buying: about 1 in 3 (33% in 2020; 37% of concluded searches in 2024). Since about 2014 only around 57% of recent searchers buy.
- Of companies acquired (2024): about 69% gain, 31% loss. Of the losses, about a third are total losses.
- Bands among companies with a gain (2024): 1 to 2x about 27%, 2 to 5x about 36%, 5 to 10x about 25%, over 10x about 11%.
- Big winners take time: 10x+ exits were held about 10 years on average; 1 to 2x exits about 6 years.
- Time from start of search to close: median 19 months over all deals (about 23 months in recent cohorts); 16% close in under 11 months, 18% take over 30 months.
- Recent searchers sign about 3.6 LOIs on average, the first about 8 months into the search (2024). Failed LOIs: diligence findings, seller retreat, investor support, valuation.
- Deep-dive (industry-focused) searches identified a median of about 140 targets; high-volume searches about 500 (2020). Deep-dive is now the majority approach.

## Median deal metrics (search-acquired companies)

| Metric | All deals (2024 study) | 2018-19 deals | 2022-23 deals |
|---|---|---|---|
| Purchase price | $12.8M | $10.0M | $14.4M |
| Revenue | $7.3M | $6.3M | $6.7M |
| EBITDA | $1.9M | $1.8M | $2.2M |
| EBITDA margin | about 23% | 21% | 27% |
| EBITDA growth at purchase | about 11% | 15% | 25% |
| Price / EBITDA | 6.3x to 6.4x | 6.0x | 7.0x |
| Price / revenue | 1.8x | 1.4x | 1.9x |
| Employees | 40 | 32 | 34 |

- Software deals pushed multiples up in 2020-21 (7.3x); fewer software deals in 2022-23 pulled them back. Recent acquisitions skew to services, tech-enabled services and healthcare services.
- Only about 35% of searchers buy in the state where they searched (2024): plan to relocate.

## Fundraising and pay

- Search capital raised: median about $450K to $550K per fund (about $500K per principal in 2022-23); median 12 to 15 investors; median 3 months to raise.
- At acquisition, median 16 investors: about 12 from the search round plus 4 new.
- Search-period salary: mean about $139K (2022-23 cohort; about $120K the cohort before).
- First-year CEO pay: median base about $190K to $200K plus about $25K target bonus; rises to roughly $260K to $290K total by years 2 to 4 (2024).
- Equity earned by searcher-CEOs (2024): mean about $6M, median about $2M per person; 2020 study per-person mean about $4.4M while operating.

## Solo versus partnered

- Partnered searches have higher pooled IRR (about 40% vs 30% in 2024; about 40% vs 27% in 2020), but the share of solo searchers with 5x+ outcomes has risen, and most recent 10x+ outcomes were solo.
- Partnerships fell to about 19% of new searches in 2022-23.
- Typical searcher equity: solo up to about 25%, pair about 30% combined.

## Sellers

- Median seller age about 55; sellers come from every age band, so do not assume "retiring founder" (2020, 2024).
- Sellers stayed engaged a median of about 6 months after close (2024, up from 4); over 90% of searchers reported a good post-close relationship; about 12% keep the seller involved indefinitely.

## Variant models (2024 study, Appendix C)

- **Self-funded search:** searcher pays own costs, uses more debt, buys smaller companies, ends with about 50% to 70% ownership. Most numerous model.
- **Single-investor, accelerator or entrepreneur-in-residence:** one backer pays salary and gives shared services (training, databases, bank and legal introductions); searcher economics similar to core funds.
- **Long-term hold:** larger capital committed at launch, narrow thesis, 10 to 20 year intended hold, growth mostly by acquisition, board with capital-call powers, CEO judged on capital allocation. Tracked separately from 2024.
