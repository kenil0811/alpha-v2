# Independent sponsors, family offices and long-hold capital

Drawn from practitioner decks (family-office and holding-company investors, 2020 to 2025; an independent-sponsor and family-office direct-investing deck, 2023 to 2025; a lower-middle-market fund deck, 2021) and a 2018 Bain note on longer holding periods. Own words.

## Vehicles side by side

| Vehicle | Capital | Typical horizon | Edge with sellers | Watch-outs |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Traditional PE fund | Committed LP fund, 10-year life | 3 to 5 years | Certainty, speed, lender relationships | Must sell on the fund clock; IRR focus |
| Independent (fundless) sponsor | Raised deal by deal from family offices, mezzanine funds, individuals | Varies | Flexibility, focus | No committed equity: seller and lender doubt certainty of close; non-circumvention NDAs |
| Single family office | One family's money, often direct | Long, sometimes permanent | Patient, flexible, can skip financing contingency | Can be slow, under-resourced, family priorities first |
| Multi-family office | Several families pooled | Long | As above, bigger cheques | Governance across families |
| Long-hold fund | Committed fund with 10 to 15+ year life | 10 to 20 years | Founder-friendly, fewer resales | Duration risk; needs periodic re-diligence |
| Permanent holding company | Balance-sheet capital, shareholders not LPs | Indefinite | "Company first" pitch; no forced sale | Hard to show liquidity to investors |
| Search fund (see main skill) | Search capital then deal equity | 5 to 10 years | Owner-operator successor | First-time CEO risk |

## Pitching a family office or holdco to a founder

- Not a fund: no forced sale date; decisions in the company's long-term interest.
- Flexible structures (minority, majority, rollover, staged); no financing contingency even if some leverage is used.
- Operating help: M&A support, financing advice, recruiting, technology and cyber roadmaps, talent development.
- Typical lower-middle-market holdco box seen in the decks: about $4M to $20M EBITDA, $25M to $250M revenue, $10M to $100M equity per deal, niche B2B manufacturing, specialty distribution, business services, food. A distribution-only family holdco sought $5M to $30M EBITDA, complex, high-SKU businesses.
- Survey data quoted in one deck (around 2020): about half of family offices prefer direct cheques of $0.5M to $5M; only a few percent above $100M. So small deals are where family-office money is most available to independent sponsors.

## Independent sponsor checklist (standard practice)

1. Thesis first: what do the equity providers want (return target, risk, cash yield vs growth, hold period); how will returns be made (multiple arbitrage through add-ons, revenue and margin growth, debt paydown); what is your edge (operating skill, M&A machine, lower return hurdle, risk appetite).
2. Lock the target with an LOI before raising equity; line up a lead capital partner early so the seller believes you can close.
3. Agree sponsor economics with the capital partner: closing fee, ongoing management or monitoring fee, and carried interest that steps up with returns. Put them in the term sheet with the equity partner before diligence spending starts.
4. Budget dead-deal costs; you carry them without a fund.

## Long-hold economics

- Hard gains from holding longer: fewer transaction costs, capital stays invested, capital gains tax deferred, sale timed to the market rather than the fund.
- Softer gains: management not distracted by resales; time to buy and integrate add-ons, roll out technology and talent programmes, make investments with slow payback.
- Bain (2018) modelled one company held 24 years against four successive 6-year buyouts with the same operating results: the long hold returned almost twice as much after tax. Bain also cited "core" long-hold funds targeting 12% to 14% IRR with lower fees.
- Discipline: re-diligence each holding every 3 to 4 years (technology, regulation, competition); sell if duration risk has grown; otherwise reset the value-creation plan.

## Small-fund leverage note

- One lower-middle-market fund used an SBIC licence: SBA leverage of about 1:1 on private capital via 10-year, interest-only, fixed-rate debentures, which lets a small fund pay a yield and still lever equity returns. Its box: Midwest, family-owned succession deals, $2M to $10M EBITDA, margins above 10%.

## Exit planning for long holders (from the same 2021 deck)

Plan backwards from the exit: what must be in place for the next buyer (clean accounting, known value drivers, management depth and succession, contract and lease renewals) and start about 18 months before a sale. Assess timing continuously, since exit multiples swing widely across the cycle.
