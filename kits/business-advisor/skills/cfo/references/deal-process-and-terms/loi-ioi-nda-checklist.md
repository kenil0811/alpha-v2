# Teaser, NDA, IOI and LOI: what each contains

Section lists built from a sample teaser, mutual confidentiality agreement, indication of interest and letter of intent (one fictional buyer, one fictional family seller, asset deal around 6x EBITDA). Use them as checklists when drafting or reviewing.

## Teaser
One or two anonymous pages from the seller's adviser: what the business does, highlights, summary financials (often a chart), region rather than city, almost nothing on management, and the adviser's contacts. Its job is to let a buyer judge fit before signing an NDA.

## NDA (confidentiality agreement)
Check that it covers:
- Definition of confidential information, with the usual exclusions (public through no fault of recipient, received from a free source, already known with written proof, independently developed).
- Use only to evaluate the deal; no disclosure except to people who need to know and are bound in writing.
- Return or destruction on request.
- No obligation to proceed with a deal; no reps about the information.
- Governing law, severability, entire agreement, written amendments.
- **Term:** the sample has no end date (obligations last until the information becomes public). Buyers normally negotiate a term of one to three years (standard practice).
- **Missing from the sample but common** (standard practice): non-solicitation of the target's employees, no contact with customers, suppliers or staff without permission, standstill (for public targets), and for fundless sponsors a non-circumvention clause.

## IOI (indication of interest)
One or two pages, answering the bid letter's questions in order:
1. Price as a range (dollars or a multiple of stated EBITDA) and what it is based on (the sample: TTM EBITDA, CIM adjustments taken at face value).
2. Structure: asset or stock; working-capital peg "to be negotiated"; any rollover offered to the family (the sample offered up to 20% of Newco).
3. Seller's role after closing: paid consultant for a period; non-compete, non-solicit, non-disparagement.
4. Management: who stays, on employment contracts with restrictive covenants.
5. Funding: named senior lender, the fund's committed capital, possible co-investors.
6. Real estate: market-rate lease if the owner holds the property.
7. Timing: weeks to a full LOI after management meetings, then weeks to close.
8. Contingencies: lender approval, investment committee, third-party QofE.
9. Key assumptions the price depends on.
10. Non-binding language, confidentiality of the letter, expiry date.

## LOI (letter of intent)
Three to fifteen pages. The sample's sections, in order:
1. **Valuation and structure:** firm price; cash-free, debt-free; working-capital peg as a dollar figure (LTM average) with a written definition (net receivables excluding a named bad debt, plus inventory, less payables) and reserve adjustments; true-up within 90 days; form of consideration (cash, seller note, rollover); escrow amount; price allocation to be set during diligence; sources and uses table.
2. **Senior loans:** revolver draw and term loan amounts, maturity, amortisation, first lien, "market" covenants.
3. **Seller note:** maturity, rate, second lien, subordinated, payment schedule (the sample: three years, interest quarterly, half the principal at year two and half at maturity).
4. **Equity:** sponsor and rollover units ranking equally.
5. **Owner transition:** short consulting agreement with hours and fees stepping down, restrictive covenants, assignment of all IP to Newco.
6. **Diligence and timing:** scope (regulatory, financial, commercial, tax, legal, environmental), access to advisers, suppliers and selected customers, required consents; target close within 90 days; sign and close at the same time; each side pays its own costs.
7. **Definitive agreement terms:** approvals; customary reps and indemnities; escrow amount and period (the sample: about 10% of price for nine months); knowledge defined as the owner's after due inquiry; matters outside the escrow cap (title, authority, non-compete breach, fraud, broker fees, retained liabilities, tax, intentional breaches); all shareholders join the indemnity.
8. **No-shop and confidentiality:** exclusivity for a set period (the sample: 90 days, automatically extended 30 days while both sides negotiate in good faith); seller must report any approach within 48 hours.
9. **Governing law and jury waiver.**
10. **Announcements:** mutual consent, with carve-outs for investors, lenders and advisers.
11. **Nature of the letter:** non-binding except no-shop, confidentiality, governing law and announcements.
12. Expiry time and signature blocks.

## Review questions before sending an LOI
- Does the price state the EBITDA it rests on and which adjustments are assumed?
- Is working capital defined, not just pegged?
- Are seller note terms and subordination stated, so the senior lender will accept them?
- Is exclusivity long enough for QofE, legal work and lender approval (60 to 90 days is typical; standard practice)?
- Are uncapped matters listed now, while the buyer still has leverage?
- Does the owner's transition role match what the 100-day plan needs?
