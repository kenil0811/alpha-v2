# Asset purchase agreement: clause map

What each part of a typical buyer-drafted asset purchase agreement (APA) does, and what a buyer pushes for. Built from a sample APA (a two-seller, two-buyer-entity widget business, signed and closed the same day) and a standard appendix of typical reps and covenants. Sizes quoted from the sample are one data point, not a market norm.

## How to read one
1. Read the definitions article first. Capitalised words carry the deal: "Business EBITDA", "Knowledge", "Losses", "Material Adverse Effect", "Fundamental Representations", "Working Capital", "Escrow Amount".
2. Then read price (article 3) and indemnification (article 8) together: they decide how much is paid, when, and how much can come back.
3. Then read the seller reps against the disclosure schedules, one by one.

## Article by article

| Article | What it does | Buyer negotiates for |
|---|---|---|
| Recitals | Names the parties and the business; in an asset deal the buyer is usually a new entity ("Newco") per business line | Separate Newcos per line if the businesses will be financed or sold separately |
| 1 Definitions and interpretation | Fixes the meaning of every key term | A broad "Losses" (including diminution in value and advisers' fees); a "Knowledge" that means a named person's actual knowledge plus what they would know after reasonable inquiry; an earn-out EBITDA defined from audited statements and excluding the earn-out itself |
| 2 Purchase and sale | Lists purchased assets, excluded assets (seller's cash, tax refunds, corporate records, named contracts and vehicles), assumed liabilities (listed payables, post-closing contract duties) and retained liabilities | Assumed liabilities kept to a short closed list; everything else retained by the seller, named explicitly: debt, pre-closing employment and benefit claims, environmental, taxes, deal fees, pre-closing contract breaches, lawsuits and product liability from before closing; property taxes prorated to the closing date |
| 3 Purchase price | Headline price, plus or minus the working-capital adjustment, plus earn-outs, plus assumed liabilities; closing cash is the headline less seller debt payoffs, seller deal costs and the escrow deposit | See price mechanics below |
| 4 Seller reps and warranties | Statements of fact by the sellers and the owner, jointly and severally, qualified only by numbered schedules | See rep list below; the owner personally on the hook, not just the selling entity |
| 5 Buyer reps | Organisation, authority, no conflict, consents, no litigation blocking the deal, brokers | Keep it this short; buyers rarely give more (in a stock deal, add an investment-intent rep) |
| 6 Closing and deliveries | Where and when closing happens, and what each side hands over | Seller deliveries: bills of sale, IP assignments, good-standing and tax-clearance certificates, secretary's certificates, signed escrow agreement, new leases (often from the owner's property company), lender payoff letters and lien releases, required third-party consents, the owner's employment agreement, the owner's rollover signature into Newco |
| 7 Post-closing covenants | Further assurances, announcements, name change, transfer taxes, access to records, misdirected payments, non-compete, non-solicit, confidentiality, remedies | Non-compete and non-solicit long enough to protect goodwill (the sample used five years, nationwide plus named competitors and customers), with a small public-stock carve-out; injunctions without proof of damages; a court allowed to narrow rather than strike an over-broad clause |
| 8 Survival and indemnification | How long reps last, who pays for what, procedure, limits, exclusive remedy | See indemnity mechanics below |
| 9 Miscellaneous | Expenses, notices, entire agreement, assignment, governing law, venue, jury waiver, severability, equitable relief, counterparts | Right to assign to lenders and to a later buyer of the business |
| Exhibits and schedules | Price allocation, dispute procedure (independent accountant), leases, employment agreement, every disclosure schedule | Schedules delivered early enough to diligence them |

## Price mechanics worth copying
- **Working-capital adjustment:** a fixed target (the LTM average); closing working capital is book current assets minus book current liabilities under GAAP, excluding excluded assets. The buyer prepares the closing balance sheet within about 90 days; the seller has about 30 days to object; disputes go to an independent accountant. A shortfall can be recovered from escrow, set off against earn-outs, or billed to the seller, at the buyer's choice.
- **Earn-out:** paid as a multiple of EBITDA above a threshold, tiered and capped per year (the sample paid a lower multiple on the first slice above the threshold and a higher one above that, with an annual cap). Same 30-day calculate, 30-day object, accountant-decides procedure. The buyer promises not to act to avoid the earn-out but keeps full control of operations and no duty to maximise it. The earn-out is forfeited if the owner quits without good reason (pro rata for the current year).
- **Escrow:** treated as the seller's money for tax; released after a set period (nine months in the sample) unless claims are open; forfeited if the owner quits without good reason.
- **Allocation:** the price split across asset classes for tax is agreed in an exhibit and binds both sides' tax returns.

## Indemnity mechanics worth copying
- **Survival:** ordinary reps survive to a fixed date (about 9 to 18 months is common; the sample used about nine); fundamental reps survive longer (authority, organisation and capitalisation, title, IP, tax, brokers).
- **Scope of seller indemnity:** rep breaches, covenant breaches, retained liabilities, broker claims, claims by former equity holders, and anything arising from pre-closing operations.
- **Basket:** claims start only once a threshold is passed. The sample used a "tipping" basket (once crossed, recovery runs from the first dollar), which favours the buyer; a "deductible" basket (only the excess) favours the seller.
- **Order of recovery:** escrow first, then set-off against earn-outs, then direct claims against the sellers; the buyer may withhold an earn-out while a claim is pending.
- **Carve-outs:** limits do not apply to fundamental reps, the owner's employment agreement, or fraud and wilful misconduct.
- **Pro-sandbagging:** the buyer's diligence knowledge does not reduce its claim.
- **Exclusive remedy** for everything except fraud and injunctions.
- **Cap:** the sample sets none for ordinary reps beyond the escrow-first order; a seller will ask for one (often 10% to 20% of price; standard practice).
- **Tax treatment:** indemnity payments are price adjustments.

## Seller reps to expect (checklist)
Authority and no conflict; required consents; organisation, standing and capitalisation; financial statements fairly presented; capital leases; receivables valid and collectible (with ageing); payables listed; work in process and its revenue recognition; internal controls and no fraud; no undisclosed liabilities; debt listed; title to and sufficiency and condition of assets; subsidiaries; litigation; compliance with law; no material adverse change and ordinary-course conduct since the last balance sheet; supplier stability; a negative list of things not done since that date; IP; environmental and safety; top customers (the sample listed the 20 largest); affiliate transactions; material contracts; taxes; employment; benefit plans; insurance; product warranties; brokers; books and records; solvency after closing; full disclosure.

## Interim covenants (only when signing and closing are separated)
Preserve the assets; no new long-term or material contracts; no hiring or firing outside the plan; no new debt; no share issues; capex under a cap; best efforts to close and obtain consents; no-shop; access for the buyer; notice of significant events.

## Signs of a seller-favourable draft
Knowledge qualifiers everywhere; materiality scrapes missing; a deductible basket and low cap; short survival; no owner joinder; earn-out with a buyer duty to run the business to hit it; escrow released on a fixed date regardless of open claims.
