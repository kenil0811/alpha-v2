Reference ranges collected from lender decks and market reports, each tagged with the year it reflects. Rates are spreads over the base rate of the time (LIBOR, "L", before about 2022; SOFR, "S", after). Always refresh with live lender quotes; these show shape, not today's price.

## Whole-package limits (lower middle market, bank era)
Senior debt at most 2.5-3.5x EBITDA; total debt at most 3.5-5x; FCCR at least about 1.2x; equity at least 25% plus 5 points per turn of purchase multiple over 4x, capped at 50%. In the late 2010s the typical buyout was about half debt (plus or minus 5 points), with middle-market deals using a similar share despite lower prices.

## Direct lenders versus banks
- After 2008, bank rules limited leveraged loans (roughly above 3x leverage drew scrutiny), and regulated banks' share of leveraged financing fell from about half to well under a fifth by the early 2020s (2024 data).
- Direct lenders charged about 5-6 points over the base rate, roughly 2-3 points more than banks, plus higher fees (2024).
- Private credit grew from about $0.3T (2010) to about $1.7T (2025) (2026 market data). Private-debt assets passed $1.2T in 2022, with direct lending the largest strategy (roughly a third or more of fundraising) (2022-2023 market reports).
- Average annual credit-loss rates, 2012-2022: private credit about 0.9%, high-yield bonds about 1.4%, syndicated loans about 0.8% (2026 data from an index provider).
- When markets turn, liquid leveraged loans fall and recover faster than private-debt funds; defaults increasingly happen as "distressed exchanges" (a sponsor-negotiated swap) rather than missed payments (2023 report).
- Syndicated LBO loan yields reached about 10% in late 2022 as base rates rose; private-debt funds returned about 8% over the year to Q3 2023 (2022-2023 reports).

## Lender process alongside the sale process
1. Before IOIs: the buyer (or the seller's banker) asks lenders for leverage and pricing guidance.
2. Lenders screen internally.
3. IOI stage: lenders give indicative term sheets supporting a buyer's bid.
4. Check-in bids: buyers and lenders refresh value and structure.
5. LOI stage: lenders issue committed financing papers.
6. Purchase agreement signed; loan documents signed and funded at closing.

A lender's committee wants: transaction overview (sources and uses, cap table, pro forma ownership); a supportable valuation and the drivers to monitor; company and market overview; key risks to enterprise value and cash flow; leverage, loan-to-value and FCCR at close and in a downside case; covenant set (leverage, EBITDA floor, FCCR); legal, environmental, customer calls.

## Tracking the capital structure after close
Keep a capitalisation table with columns for each event (original close, recap or add-on, today) and, per tranche, committed amount, funded amount, cumulative leverage multiple and loan-to-value. Include earn-outs and lease liabilities as junior obligations even if the credit agreement excludes them from covenant leverage. Show pro forma LTM adjusted EBITDA (including acquired EBITDA), balance-sheet cash, and senior and total net leverage; note how much cash the agreement lets you net.

## Research and cautions
- Easier credit goes with higher deal prices and lower fund returns: buyers overpay when debt is cheap (academic study, 2013).
- Securitisation (CLO demand) lowered spreads and loosened covenants in the 2000s boom, yet those deals did not underperform (academic study, 2011).
- Late in a cycle, refinancing risk rises; prefer returns from operations over financial engineering (asset manager outlook, 2019).
- A company in secular decline carrying LBO debt cannot fund the changes it needs (as a large toy retailer found). Size debt so the business can still invest.
