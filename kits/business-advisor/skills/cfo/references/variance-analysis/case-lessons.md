# Variance analysis: lessons from teaching cases

Part of `cfo/references/variance-analysis.md`. Generalised lessons from three cost-control cases.
No case answers are given.

## A traditional plant run on labour variances (an engine-plant case)

- **How the system works:** daily reports compare actual direct and indirect labour hours with the
  hours authorised for the parts made; weekly and monthly reports do the same in dollars against
  a flexible ("authorised") budget and a calendar budget; next year's budget starts from last
  year's, adjusted for volume, mix and a set improvement target.
- **What goes wrong:**
  - Managers manage the report rather than the work: holding indirect staff below the allowance
    by not covering absences, or buying in small lots to stay inside a month's budget.
  - Daily variances are mostly noise (a late truck, a bad batch of parts) and produce excuses,
    not fixes.
  - Spending more in one account to save more in another (a dearer coolant that lasts far
    longer) shows as an unfavourable variance even though total cost falls.
  - Materials, the biggest cost, are set by purchasing, so the plant is judged on the costs it
    controls least well.
  - Labour standards loaded with extra tasks over the years become unreachable.
- **What works better:** weekly or monthly trends; cost per unit and units per person compared
  with sister plants; scrap per unit by cause; tying budgets to specific cost-reduction plans
  with owners and dates.

## A team-based, automated plant (a sister engine-plant case)

- **Labour is a fixed cost here.** People are not sent home when volume dips; they are expected to
  solve problems. Labour efficiency variances lose meaning.
- **Measure the drivers of cost instead:** hourly output against plan, machine uptime, bottleneck
  operations, scrap by fault code and part, quality complaints with a 24-hour action plan.
- **Overhead per unit as a target,** shown to each team along with costs other plants treat as
  fixed (salaried support, utilities), so teams see them.
- **No rework, no incoming inspection:** defective supplier parts are charged back to the supplier
  at the full loss they cause, and in-house defects are scrapped and charged to the team.
- **Finance's role changes:** from explaining yesterday's variances to supplying operational data
  teams can act on. Strict budget pressure in the old system had blocked spending that paid back
  within months.
- Fit the control system to the operation: stable, labour-paced work suits standards and
  variances; automated, team-run, improving work suits operational measures plus cost trends.

## Standards set far below normal effort (a home-products division case)

- **The setup:** labour standards set decades ago at about a third of what a worker could do, so
  workers ran at about 300% of standard and earned a bonus per point above 100%. Overhead was
  charged on labour dollars, so it was inflated too, by different amounts in different lines.
- **Consequences:**
  - Product costs were wrong, and wrong unevenly: lines with more labour looked far costlier than
    they were. Special-quote prices built from these costs could be too high or too low.
  - Large favourable variances built up in inventory during the year and were released at year
    end to absorb unfavourable items (scrap, shrinkage, mis-reported hours). Head office could
    not tell what was left until the physical count: the year-end close became the one that
    mattered most.
  - The cushion let the division smooth earnings toward monthly targets.
  - Because standards were tied to pay, changing them meant reopening pay; because the
    manufacturing side, not accounting, owned the standards, and accounting reported to the
    division, head office had little leverage.
- **Remedy:** separate the costing standard (realistic, current effort) from the incentive
  baseline; record scrap and shrinkage as they happen; restate product costs before using them
  for pricing or dropping products; make the case to line managers in terms of pricing errors,
  not accounting purity.
