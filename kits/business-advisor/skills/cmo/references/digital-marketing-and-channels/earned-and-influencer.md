# Earned marketing, influencers and PR

Part of `cmo/references/digital-marketing-and-channels.md`.

## Why earned first for new products
Paid reach buys attention, not trust. Early buyers look for proof from others. The five trust levers (from diffusion research): certification, warranty, observability, word of mouth and trialability. Pick at least two before launch: for example an independent test plus a free trial, or a guarantee plus seeding product with visible early users.

## Goal by tactic
| Tactic | Primary goal | Who speaks |
|---|---|---|
| PR (traditional and digital) | Credibility through third-party validation | Journalists, outlets |
| Social media | Attention and engagement | The brand and others |
| Content marketing | Value that deepens understanding | The brand, sometimes with partners |
| Creators and influencers | Reach and trust within a niche | Creators |
| Referral and community | Word of mouth at low cost | Customers |
Activations: sponsored content, co-created content, guest writing, link building, product seeding, events, user-generated content prompts.

## Content types
| Type | What it is | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Library | Evergreen how-to, tools, calculators, quizzes, original research | Long-term search traffic and links |
| Journalistic | Thought leadership, timely trends, seasonal | Press pickup, authority |
| Narrative | Brand, mission and customer stories | Emotional resonance, story pitches |
| Culture and community | Trends, challenges, prompts for response | Engagement and sharing |

## Choosing conduits
Tiers run from mega (celebrities) and macro to mid and micro creators and everyday peers. Bigger tiers cost more time and money and buy awareness; smaller ones buy trust and relevance. Celebrity works when persona, values and the campaign goal align, and when it does not overshadow the brand.

Screening card for each candidate:
| Criterion | Question |
|---|---|
| Engagement | Likes, comments and shares relative to followers; are comments real conversation? |
| Audience fit | Does the audience match the segment's traits? Overlap with other creators already booked? |
| Content fit | Does tone and style suit the brand; strong on the platform the campaign uses? |
| Authenticity | Genuine or overly promotional; selective about partners; worked with rivals? |
| Credibility | Expertise in the category; track record of delivery |
| Influence type | Drives awareness, consideration or action; builds dialogue or broadcasts? |
| Results | Consistent visibility in past work; satisfaction of past partners |
| Price | Quoted fee per deliverable; compare cost per engaged follower, since fees at equal follower counts can differ several-fold |
Tools exist to find who the segment follows, reads and listens to; start from the segment's media habits, not a creator database.

**4 R's:** research (real and fit), relevance (natural to product and brand), relationships (both sides gain, ongoing), returns (measured against the goal).

## Measuring earned
Expected traces: branded searches, direct visits, referral visits and backlinks, follows, comments and shares, mentions (use a listening tool). Compute cost per customer by tactic and compare with what paid media would have cost for the same visitors at the observed conversion rate. In one subscription-rental launch, earned and referral tactics at launch delivered members at a small fraction of the forecast paid cost; repeat that comparison with your own data before scaling paid.

## Social listening and response
Monitor mentions of the brand, rivals, key terms and key accounts; track sentiment and share of voice. Have a policy for who replies and how fast; never disable or delete comments; escalate negative spikes to `crisis-communication`.
