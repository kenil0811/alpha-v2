# Web properties that convert

Part of `cmo/references/digital-marketing-and-channels.md`.

## Pre-launch checklist
- **Readiness:** responsive (mobile-first), HTTPS, fast on mobile networks, accessible (alt text, captions, contrast, keyboard use), privacy and cookie consent compliant.
- **Measurement:** analytics installed, conversion goals defined, ad pixels and tags firing, social accounts linked to site analytics, tagged links (source, medium, campaign) for every campaign.
- **Brand:** same name, logo, colours, type and voice across site and every social handle; register the obvious domain variants.
- **Search:** site submitted to the search engines' webmaster consoles, XML sitemap, weak pages kept out of the index, titles and meta descriptions per page, no duplicate content.
- **Soft launch:** go live quietly, prove stability, then promote; use PR at launch to earn the first inbound links.

## Hierarchy and page jobs
| Level | Example | Job | Funnel stage |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Home | Hub | Orient, route by need or segment | Explore |
| 2 Category | "Kitchen sinks" | Help narrow choices | Research |
| 3 Sub-category | "Farmhouse sinks" | Match a specific need; often what ranks in search | Research and compare |
| 4 Product or service | One item | Prove and persuade | Compare and decide |
| Resource pages | Guides, tools, calculators | Earn trust, capture long-tail search | Explore and research |
| Landing pages | Campaign-specific | Convert one audience on one promise | Decide |
Fewer choices per level reduces cognitive load. Granularity should follow search demand: if a sub-topic has its own large question volume, give it its own page.

## Three questions for each page's storyboard 
What action should be taken? Who must take it? How do we persuade that person (without misleading them)?

## Card sorting (structure the menu with users)
1. Brainstorm every content idea users would expect, one per card.
2. Let users (or the team acting as users) group cards without labels; set aside misfits.
3. Name the level-2 navigation categories and distinct, not-too-deep sub-categories.
4. Prioritise content within each: must, should, could, won't have.
5. Map the result to a site map (blueprint), then wireframes, then test with users.

## Navigation and links
Navigational menus, utility links (account, cart, contact), quick links, contextual in-text links to the next logical step, footer links, breadcrumbs. Interlinking helps users and search engines alike.

## Design rules
About five colours at most, three typefaces in three sizes, purposeful images, consistent components. Content strategy covers: what you sell, content about you, content about them (value-add guides, tools and research), and content made with them (reviews, user stories).

## Marketplace listings (selling through partners)
Study each partner's page anatomy: image carousel and video, title, ratings, "about this item" bullets, enhanced brand content below the fold. Balance functional detail (ingredients, sizes, instructions) with emotional and lifestyle content. Ratings, fast shipping badges, bundle packs and subscribe-and-save options affect both conversion and the marketplace's ranking algorithm.

## Ongoing
Update content regularly; test landing pages (A/B on headline, offer, sign-up incentive); use exit-intent surveys to learn why visitors leave; watch bounce rate by landing page and speed by device.
