# Strategic communication plan and launch messaging

> **Area:** `strategic-communication-plan` · owned by the CMO skill. **Use when:** Build a company's strategic communication plan (strategy, vision-mission-values, stakeholder map, SWOT, message map) and a launch campaign with talking points and stakeholder Q&A. Use when a business announces something, launches a product, or needs one consistent story across employees, investors, customers and media.

**Outcome:** one agreed set of messages, mapped to each stakeholder, that leaders can deliver
consistently in a launch, an announcement or an executive speech.

## When to use
- A launch, rebrand, new leader, expansion, price change or other announcement.
- Leaders say different things to different audiences.
- Preparing talking points, a press release, an all-hands speech or investor remarks.
- Before any crisis: the same plan is the base the crisis plan builds on (see `crisis-communication`).

## Method

### 1. Company strategy on one page
- **Vision:** where the company is going (5-10 years), one sentence.
- **Mission:** what it does, for whom, and how, today.
- **Values:** 3-5 behaviours that guide decisions, each with a plain example.
- **Strategy:** the 2-4 choices that win (where to play, how to win, what you will not do).
- Check against public facts: annual report, recent news releases, leadership changes,
  industry outlooks. Messages that contradict the record fail.

### 2. Stakeholder identification and analysis
1. List every group affected or able to affect the outcome: employees (by level and site),
   customers, shareholders or owners, lenders, suppliers, regulators, community and local
   officials, media, partners, advocacy groups.
2. For each: their interest in this news, what they fear, what you need them to do, their
   influence (high/low) and their current attitude (support/neutral/oppose).
3. Prioritise on a power-interest grid (standard practice): manage
   high-power high-interest closely; keep high-power low-interest satisfied; keep
   low-power high-interest informed; monitor the rest.
4. Choose channel and messenger per group (employees hear first, from their own leaders).

### 3. SWOT that feeds messages
- Strengths and weaknesses are internal; opportunities and threats are external (market
  trends, competitors, regulation, consumer shifts).
- For each item, write the communication consequence: which strength becomes a proof point,
  which weakness needs a prepared answer, which threat may become a crisis.

### 4. Message map
A message map is a one-page hierarchy every spokesperson uses (structure is standard practice):
- **Overarching message:** one sentence the audience should remember.
- **Three key messages:** the reasons that support it (three is easy to recall).
- **Three proof points per key message:** facts, numbers, examples, third-party endorsements.
- Keep each key message short enough to say in about ten seconds.
- Tailor the order and proof points by stakeholder; never change the core.

### 5. Marketing and launch plan
- Objective (measurable), target segments, positioning statement, offer.
- **Campaign launch strategy:** phases (tease, launch, sustain), channels, timing, budget,
  owners, success metrics.
- **Launch talking points:** 5-8 bullet lines drawn from the message map, for leaders and
  front-line staff.
- **Stakeholder Q&A:** 3-5 likely questions each from employees, shareholders and media,
  with the answer and the bridge back to a key message. Include the hard ones (jobs, costs,
  risks, why now).

### 6. Executive message (speech)
- Length: 4-5 minutes for a press conference statement.
- Organise from the message map: open with the overarching message, one section per key
  message with its proof, close by repeating the core and a call to action.
- Use at least four deliberate rhetorical strategies: for example a story or example,
  contrast, repetition of the key line, rhetorical question, inclusive "we", appeal to shared
  values, data with a human face.
- Rehearse and record; review both verbal (clarity, pace, filler words) and non-verbal
  (eye contact, posture, gestures, voice) delivery.

### 7. Brand and format
- Deliverables look like business documents: logo, brand colours and font from the style
  guide, headings, short paragraphs.
- State any AI assistance where the organisation's policy expects it.

## Worked pattern
A regional discount retailer appoints its first CEO from outside the founding family.
Overarching message: "We are keeping the values that built us and adding the skills to grow."
Key messages: (1) values continuity, proof: founder family remains on board, 75 years of
community giving, sustainability ranking; (2) new capabilities, proof: CEO's 20 years in
sports marketing, a new loyalty programme, three regional hires; (3) commitment to people,
proof: no planned layoffs, 15,000 local jobs, a leadership programme for women.
Employees hear it first in a town hall, then shareholders by letter, then the media release.
Prepared hard question: "What does a sports marketer know about retail?" Answer with the
second key message and bridge back to the first.

## Common mistakes
- A different story per audience instead of one core with tailored proof.
- Too many messages; nobody remembers the fourth one.
- Proof points that are adjectives, not facts.
- Employees learning news from the media.
- Q&A that skips the questions leaders fear most.
- Ignoring the record (recent awards, leadership changes, annual report) that reporters will check.
