# Digital marketing, channels and the growth engine

> **Area:** `digital-marketing-and-channels` · owned by the CMO skill. **Use when:** Plan and run how a business reaches, persuades, converts and keeps customers: a full-funnel "growth engine" across paid, owned and earned channels; targeting by audience, life event, context and search intent; websites and digital properties that convert; search, social, email, influencer and PR tactics; campaign design; and the funnel metrics (ROAS, CPC, conversion, lead scores, NPS) that show what works. Use when someone asks "where should we spend our marketing budget?", "why isn't the website converting?", "should we do ads, SEO, influencers or PR?" or "is this campaign paying back?"

**Outcome:** a journey map with one goal, channel, message and metric per stage; a budget whose return target is set from margin; properties built to convert; and a short list of metrics that show whether awareness, trial and repeat are moving.

## When to use
- Launching a product with little or no awareness and a small budget.
- Ads run but cost per customer is too high, or traffic comes but does not convert.
- Choosing between paid search, social, influencers, PR, email and content.
- Building or rebuilding a website, a marketplace listing or social accounts.
- Setting marketing KPIs and a dashboard; qualifying leads for a sales team.

Related: `brand-and-positioning` (the message), `market-sizing-and-unit-economics` (segments, CAC and customer value), `consumer-behavior-marketing` (attention, persuasion, testing), `market-entry-and-go-to-market` (the full go-to-market plan this sits inside), `crisis-communication` (when social turns negative), `new-product-forecasting` (ad response models).

## Method

### 1. Start from goal and budget, never from the tactic
Order of decisions: **goal → channel → tactic → targeting → creative → metric.** In practice requests arrive in the wrong order ("I made a video, where do I post it?"); rebuild the chain before spending. Use SMART goals and set the budget before tactics.

### 2. Build the growth engine (full funnel)
Five gears, each with its own job and metric:
| Gear | Job | Typical channels | Lead metric |
|---|---|---|---|
| Discovery | Create new demand, get noticed | PR, influencers, social video, display, sponsorship | Reach, branded search, share of search |
| Engagement (consideration) | Steward existing interest | Website content, email, retargeting, reviews, webinars | Engaged visits, sign-ups, return visits |
| Conversion | Capture demand already in market | Paid search, marketplace ads, landing pages, sales | Conversion rate, cost per acquisition |
| Loyalty | Keep the demand you won | Email, app, onboarding, service, loyalty offers | Repeat rate, churn |
| Advocacy | Turn customers into a channel | Referral programmes, reviews, community, UGC | Referrals, NPS, reviews |
An equivalent framework is RACE (Chaffey): reach, act, convert, engage. Why not just buy the bottom of the funnel? Those buyers are already comparing options (no room to persuade), every rival bids for them (cost rises), and a new brand is not yet trusted. A bottom-only plan is fast to start and expensive to scale.

### 3. Map intent and the journey
- **Care scale (intent stages):** no intent → researching (learn) → discovering (learn, commercial intent forms) → considering (compare) → deciding (act). Not everyone moves forward, and a **trigger** (season, life event, a problem) moves people from unengaged to engaged.
- **Triggers:** established ones repeat (seasons, holidays: a fire-pit sold in November misses its buying season); emerging ones are new trends. Check search-trend data before setting launch dates.
- **Journey map per segment:** for each stage, why they are in a channel (motivation), where (channel), what success looks like for them there, and how to meet that (message and format). The message must match the medium and the moment; not every touch is the last one, so set a goal for each.
- **Search intent mapping:** sort the segment's real queries by stage ("how to stay energised" = research; "best natural energy drink" = compare; "where to buy X" = act). The more buying intent a query shows, the more a click costs.
Template and example: `cmo/references/digital-marketing-and-channels/journey-and-channel-plan.md`.

### 4. Choose channels: paid, owned, earned
- **Paid:** you buy the placement (search ads, social ads, display, sponsorship, paid influencers). Fast and controllable; stops when spend stops; algorithms need weeks of spend to learn ("tuition").
- **Owned:** you control the asset (website, email list, app, social accounts, content). Cheap per contact, slow to build; organic social reach is now low (often a few % of followers per post).
- **Earned:** others carry your message (press, reviews, word of mouth, unpaid creators, shares). Most trusted, least controllable, often cheapest per customer early on.
- Hybrids matter: paid/earned (paying a creator whose audience then shares), owned/earned (a referral programme), paid/owned (ads to grow your list).
- A purely paid and purely owned launch rarely works for something new: relevance, resonance, reach and then trust must be built, and others' voices build trust fastest.

### 5. Target: audience, event, context, intent
- **Audiences:** demographic, affinity (interests and habits), custom (keywords, competitor sites and apps your buyer uses), in-market (recent shopping behaviour), lookalikes.
- **Life events:** moving, marriage, a baby, a new job, renovating: map them to the need triggers found in segmentation.
- **Contextual:** keyword, topic and placement targeting puts the offer where the need scenario is being read about. This is how a challenger outsmarts rivals it cannot outspend.
- **Intent:** search keywords, by stage.
- **Reach versus relevance:** each layer of targeting cuts reach; narrow = more relevant and more competitive (dearer per person); broad = cheaper reach and more waste. Start narrow on the beachhead segment, widen as creative and data prove out.

### 6. Set the return target from margin
- Break-even ROAS (return on ad spend = revenue ÷ ad spend) = 1 ÷ gross margin %. At 40% margin you need $2.50 of sales per $1 of ads just to cover product cost.
- Target ROAS = revenue goal ÷ ad budget, where the budget is a chosen share of gross profit. Example: revenue goal $100,000, margin 45%, gross profit $45,000, ads at 20% of gross profit = $9,000, so ROAS must be about 11.
- **ROAS is not ROI:** profit = revenue × margin − ad spend. A ROAS of 3 at 30% margin loses money.
- Allowable cost per acquisition: set from customer lifetime value (`market-sizing-and-unit-economics`), not from the first order alone.

### 7. Run the main tactics well
- **Paid search:** bid on key phrases; the platform's quality score (expected click-through, ad and keyword relevance, landing-page relevance, speed) sets rank and price, so relevance beats budget. Manage bids by day and hour; it is never set-and-forget.
- **SEO and answer engines:** a clear page hierarchy, internal links, sitemap, metadata and alt text, mobile-first speed, HTTPS, fresh and original content, links from other sites. Target the long-tail questions the segment asks.
- **Display and programmatic:** bought per thousand impressions or per click across networks; measure view-through and interaction, not only clicks.
- **Email (house list):** welcome and onboarding, abandoned-cart and other triggered messages, newsletters, reactivation. Set a contact policy (frequency, interval, content, channel). Watch the "emotionally unsubscribed" who never open; offer "fewer emails" instead of a single unsubscribe.
- **Social:** be social on social. Most posts (about three in four) should be native content people would engage with anyway; the occasional sell (the "right hook") lands only after those "jabs". Never delete criticism; reply. Engagement rate drives algorithmic reach.
- **Affiliates:** commission per sale or click via tracked links; poor fit for low-margin products.
- **Earned and influencers:** see step 9.

### 8. Make every property a growth engine
Five keys for a website (and the same logic for marketplace listings and social accounts):
1. **Foundations first:** mobile-first, fast, secure, accessible, compliant, with analytics, tags and pixels installed before launch.
2. **Hierarchy:** home page as the hub, then categories, sub-categories, products; the most important content within a click or two.
3. **Every page has a job** for a stage of the journey (home and resource pages: explore; category and product pages: research and compare; landing pages: convert).
4. **Seamless navigation:** navigational, utility, contextual and footer links that lead to the next logical step; breadcrumbs.
5. **Design to engage and convert:** a restrained palette and type system tied to the brand; content that gives value at each stage (guides, tools, original research), not only products.
Ads go to a dedicated landing page that repeats the ad's promise, not to the home page. Card sorting with real users decides menus. Details and checklists: `cmo/references/digital-marketing-and-channels/web-properties.md`.

### 9. Bridge the trust gap with earned marketing
New products face five trust hurdles that marketing can lower: **certification** (a third party vouches), **warranty** (the risk moves to you), **observability** (others can see it in use), **word of mouth** and **trialability** (try before committing).
- **Content types:** library (evergreen how-to, tools, original research), journalistic (timely thought leadership, seasonal), narrative (brand and mission stories), culture and community (trends, prompts for response and user content).
- **Conduits:** pick the people who already hold the segment's attention and trust: connectors (big networks), mavens (knowledge), persuaders; from mega-celebrities to micro-creators. Celebrities buy awareness and work best when persona, values and goal align; micro-creators and peers carry trust.
- **Choosing creators:** quoted prices vary widely at the same follower count. Judge engagement rate and comment quality, audience fit and overlap, content style, authenticity, past partnerships, credibility and results, not followers.
- **The 4 R's:** research the conduit is real and fits; relevance to product and brand; reciprocal relationships; measure returns.
- **Expected effects:** more branded searches, direct visits, referral visits and links, follows and shares. Compare earned cost per customer with what paid would have cost for the same customers.
Detail: `cmo/references/digital-marketing-and-channels/earned-and-influencer.md`.

### 10. Design the campaign
- **Type:** brand-building (long-term awareness and preference; emotional, story-led; reach and recall metrics), direct-response (short-term sales; features, offer and strong call to action; keyword and retargeting; optimised on response), or brand-response (both, typical for new brands).
- **Big idea:** one original, relatable, shareable hook, congruent with the brand, visible in the first seconds: ad headline, title tag, email subject line, a video's opening frame. Lead with the problem the customer feels.
- **Integration:** consistent brand, varied offers and creative, controlled frequency and interval, sensible online and offline sequence, timed to when customers are there.
- **Return on their time:** each touch should entertain, inspire, educate or move emotionally; attention is seconds long.

### 11. Measure the few numbers that matter
- **Tracking:** tag every link (source, medium, campaign) so traffic sorts into channels; without it no channel can be judged.
- **Leading indicators** (future): share of search, branded search, engagement, repeat visits, satisfaction. **Lagging** (past): sales, profit against target, cost per acquisition, conversion.
- **Per stage:** reach and CPM (discovery); click-through, CPC, bounce, time on page (engagement); conversion rate, cost per conversion, revenue per visit (conversion); repeat rate, churn (loyalty); referrals, NPS (advocacy).
- **Attribution:** last-click credits only the final referrer and undervalues discovery channels; give assisting channels part of the credit, and de-duplicate affiliates. For hard-to-measure channels, track upper-funnel signals and revenue lift over time, and test (switch on in some regions or weeks, not others). For budget across media, fit a response model with carryover (Adstock, media mix, reverse-causality checks): `cmo/references/new-product-forecasting.md` step 9.
- **Leads (B2B):** grade fit (industry, size, location, role) A to D and score behaviour (pages read, downloads, webinars) 0 to 10; route A-high to sales, nurture the rest; zero scores after long inactivity. Marketing-qualified versus sales-qualified handoffs must be agreed.
- **NPS:** % of 9-10 scores minus % of 0-6 scores; study what made the detractors.
Formulas and a dashboard layout: `cmo/references/digital-marketing-and-channels/channel-metrics.md`; customer growth and lifetime value: `market-sizing-and-unit-economics`.

## Worked pattern
A pet-food subscription start-up, gross margin 45%, monthly marketing budget $9,000, no brand awareness.
- **Goal and return:** 300 new subscribers a month; first box $60; lifetime gross profit per subscriber about $160. Allowable acquisition cost about $50 (lifetime value ÷ 3). Break-even ROAS on first box = 1 ÷ 0.45 = 2.2.
- **Engine:** discovery via 12 micro-creators in "new puppy" content ($4,000, chosen on engagement and audience fit, not followers); engagement via a breed-specific feeding guide and quiz on the site (owned); conversion via paid search on "best food for [breed] puppy" ($3,500) to a landing page that repeats the quiz promise; loyalty via an onboarding email series; advocacy via a "give a box, get a box" referral.
- **Targeting:** life event (new pet), contextual (puppy-training content), intent (breed queries). Launch in the spring adoption season, which search trends show is the peak.
- **Result after month 2:** search: 140 subscribers, $25 each; creators: 110, $36 each; referrals: 60 at $12 (box cost). Blended: $8,180 for 310 subscribers, about $26 each, below the $50 allowance. Branded searches doubled, which last-click reports would have credited to search.
- **Decision:** keep search at its current spend (higher bids hit diminishing returns), add creators that delivered over 2% engagement, and push referrals in the second month of each subscription.

## Common mistakes
- Starting from tactics ("we need TikTok") rather than goal and budget.
- Buying only bottom-of-funnel clicks and calling it a strategy.
- Judging campaigns on ROAS without margin, or on first order without lifetime value.
- Sending ad traffic to the home page.
- Choosing influencers on follower count; using several with the same audience.
- Treating social as a broadcast channel, deleting criticism.
- Launching outside the buying season; ignoring the trigger.
- Building a beautiful site that is slow, not mobile-first or untracked.
- Trusting last-click attribution for awareness spend.
- Adopting martech because it is new, not because the strategy needs it.
