# Generalised case lessons for opportunity validation

Lessons drawn from published examples on lean start-ups, beating the odds, transformative business models
and bootstrap finance. Stated as general lessons in our own words; case facts are kept to what the lesson needs.

| Situation | What happened | Lesson |
|---|---|---|
| A clothing-rental idea | Three tests in a row: an in-person event with try-ons, one without try-ons, then an emailed catalogue to sign-ups (far lower rate, but above the pre-set pass mark) | Use a series of MVPs, each closer to the real channel; set the pass mark before the test |
| A file-sharing product used for backups | Tested demand with a demo video, not a buggy beta; kept the product simple for the mainstream and refused power-user features | Smoke-test mission-critical products; design MVPs for the eventual mainstream, not only early adopters |
| A social question-answering service | Staff routed questions by hand before any algorithm existed | Fake the operations to learn what to build |
| A tax app | Launched first for the simplest returns in one state | Constrain the customer set to cut the feature set |
| A payments start-up | Its demo website outgrew the planned device app; users then asked to show its logo on auction listings | Watch for surprises; the side use can be the business |
| A social network | Kept the feature users protested but used, dropped the one they protested and ignored | Act on revealed, not stated, preferences |
| Satellite radio for poor markets | Strong survey demand, licences won, technology solved; the receivers needed were unaffordable | Test the deal-killer (can the customer actually buy and use it?) before spending heavily |
| Medical travel abroad | Six months of interviews with benefits managers; a seminar and a few hospital calls later showed weak demand and that home hospitals cut cash prices | Run the cheap, fast test of the biggest risk first |
| An electronic-paper maker | Unsure which of three markets fitted; outsourced marketing and production and worked on the core technology for all three | Hedge path-dependent forks; keep options open until resolved |
| A joint-health drink | Heavy early spend on the wrong segment and a national grocery deal before demand data came in | Big early commitments lock in a wrong strategy; spend a little to learn a lot |
| A laundry service in a developing market | A single street kiosk tested willingness to pay within 60 days of the plan | An integrated mini-launch can test price and demand together |
| A plastics recycler | Started by reselling scrap with an outsourced grinder; each step revealed the next opportunity | Get operational quickly; opportunities appear once in the flow of business |
| An industrial-materials start-up | Investors pressed it to keep making a product the market had passed by | Early outside money can make pivots harder; validate before raising |
| Ride sharing, room sharing | Scored on several of the six transformation features (asset sharing, usage pricing, ecosystem, agility) | Score a new model against the incumbent's on the six features |
