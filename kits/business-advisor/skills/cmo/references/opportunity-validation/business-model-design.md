# Business model design: the four elements and the transformation test

Depth for `cmo/references/opportunity-validation.md` section 4. From published work on hypothesis-driven
entrepreneurship, business-model analysis, transformative business models and the start-up process; our words.

A business model is an integrated set of choices: what value the venture offers to whom, and how it
arranges activities to deliver that value and earn lasting profit. The choices must fit each other.

## The four elements and their questions

**Customer value proposition**
- Which unmet need, for which segments? Differentiation or low cost? A new market or an existing one?
- What is the MVP at launch, and the roadmap for adding features?
- Who supplies the complements needed for a whole solution, and on what terms?
- Price: skimming or penetration? Room for price discrimination, bundling, network effects?
- Switching costs customers face; expected length of a customer relationship.
- Willingness to pay against total cost of ownership, compared with rivals.

**Technology and operations**
- Activities needed to develop and produce the product; which in-house, which outsourced, to whom, on
  what terms.
- Cost drivers of key activities; scope to swap variable for fixed cost to gain scale economies.
- Valuable IP and how it stays proprietary; first-mover edges (locking up scarce inputs) and late-mover
  edges (copying).
- Can operations scale fast, given capacity and hiring limits?

**Go-to-market**
- Mix of direct and indirect channels; the margin or exclusivity partners will demand.
- Target acquisition cost given expected customer lifetime value; acquisition cost by paid method; shape of
  the conversion funnel.
- Mix of free and paid demand generation; for free methods, the viral coefficient.
- Is there a chasm between early adopters and the early mainstream, and how will it be crossed?
- Is there a reason to race for scale (network effects, high switching costs), and do scaling limits or
  late-mover advantages offset it?

**Cash flow formula**
- Contribution margin; fixed costs; break-even capacity use and sales volume.
- Break-even volume as a share of the total addressable market.
- Working capital and plant needed per dollar of revenue.
- How margins, fixed costs and investment ratios change over time.
- The cash curve: how deep the trough, and when.

## Can the model transform its industry?

New technology alone rarely transforms an industry; a business model that links it to a growing market
need does. A study of 40 new models found six recurring features; models that beat the incumbent model on
three or more tended to transform their industries, and those beating it on none rarely did.

| Feature | Meaning | Score: beats incumbent? |
|---|---|---|
| Personalisation | Offer tailored to the individual's immediate need, at a competitive price | |
| Closed loop | Used products recovered and reused, cutting resource cost | |
| Asset sharing | Costly assets shared (often via a two-sided marketplace); entrant need not own them | |
| Usage-based pricing | Customer pays when using, not to own; cost tracks value received | |
| Collaborative ecosystem | Partners share data and risk more sensibly, lowering cost | |
| Agile organisation | Decisions pushed close to the market, adapting in real time | |

How to use: define each feature in your industry's own terms (in lending, personalisation means tailored
loan terms; in education, support adjusted to each student), set a metric for each, score your model and
the dominant model, and look for features you can add. One rule-of-thumb: a model scoring 3+ is worth
pursuing as a challenger; 0 to 1 needs another source of advantage. Treat it as a screen, not a forecast:
the study found association, not proof.

## Bases of lasting advantage, and who captures the profit

Cost structure, technology, product features or quality, channels, financial resources, focus, people.
The venture must control whatever produces its unique features; if a supplier controls a critical input,
or large buyers can force uneconomic terms, they will capture the profit. Check both before committing.
