# Price corridor and business-model guide

Second and third tools from Kim and Mauborgne's blue-ocean method (the first, the buyer utility map, is in
the main file, section 6). Use them once an idea passes the utility test, before building a full plan.

## Price corridor of the mass (two steps)
1. **Find the corridor.** List the alternatives the mass of target buyers already uses, in three groups:
   same form (direct rivals); different form, same function; different form and function, same objective.
   Note each one's price and volume. The band of prices that captures the biggest groups is the corridor.
   Example: a short trip can be bought as a plane ticket or done by car, so a
   new air service's corridor runs from the cost of driving up to the airfare.
2. **Pick a level inside it** by how hard you are to copy:
   | Protection (patents, exclusive assets, brand) | Price level |
   |---|---|
   | High, hard to imitate | Upper part of the corridor |
   | Some | Middle |
   | Little, easy to imitate | Lower part, often against the cheapest substitute |

## Business-model guide
Set the cost target from the strategic price, never the price from the cost.
- **Cost target:** Is it derived from the strategic price minus the margin you need? Can cheaper, unconventional
  materials replace current ones? Can you cut or outsource high-cost, low-value activities? Can you digitise
  assets or steps? Allow no overruns; the target forces the redesign.
- **Partners:** Which capabilities does the offer need that you lack? Who has them? Buy or partner, judged on
  cost, quality and speed. Building everything yourself is slow, and imitators use the time.
- **Pricing model:** Is the industry's usual way of charging the barrier? Consider renting instead of selling,
  time-share, slice-share (sell a fraction), or taking equity or a revenue share instead of a price.

## Adoption hurdles
Before launch, list who could resist: employees whose work changes, partners whose business is threatened,
and the wider public. Plan how to bring each on board (standard framing).
