## Launch-failure check
**Market and targeting precision**
- Was real customer insight work done, or is the product a technology looking for a market?
- Is the target audience specific, reachable and prioritised?
- Is readiness to adopt (motivation, emotion, resistance) understood, or only interest?

**Clear value and position**
- Can a customer say in one sentence what sets it apart?
- Does the message link logic (outcomes) and emotion (belief)?
- Has the position been defined before the market defines it by comparison?

**The 4 Ps as one system**
- Do price, channel and promotion reinforce the position (a premium position sold through discount outlets fails)?
- Does the channel's margin, fees and returns still leave a profit per unit?
- Is the launch timed to the buying season?
- Are resources allocated to the right stage at the right time (not all budget on a splash, nothing for repeat)?

**A brand that resonates**
- Is the brand consistent, credible and fitting the target's culture?
- Is there proof before there are customers?

**Experience**
- Will the first experience deliver the promise? (A launch rarely recovers from a bad first experience.)
- Are support, onboarding and fulfilment ready for launch volume?

**Alignment (the one often missed)**
- Are the team, budget, timeline, processes and measurement framework aligned behind one plan? A strong product with a clear vision still fails when the organisation is not.

## Externalities scan
For each force (environmental, economic, social and behavioural, technological, political): the current trend, whether it helps or hurts this segment, how it changes readiness, and what would signal a change. Track public conversation by volume and emotional intensity: negative and growing is an emerging risk; positive and growing an emerging opportunity; both fading lose relevance.
