# Winning and keeping patients: what drives choice

From an advertising agency's healthcare white paper (its research dated 2021-2022).
An agency's view, so treat figures as indicative and recheck before relying on them.

## What patients weigh
- **Serious care:** perceived expertise and technology drive brand ratings, but can feel cold.
- **Routine care:** access, convenience, warmth and attentiveness drive ratings. Among "good enough" options,
  scheduling, the front desk, location and parking decide.
- **Everywhere:** being treated as a person, not a diagnosis, drives recommendation. One cited patient survey
  (2021) found the odds of a top likelihood-to-recommend score about seven times higher when patients felt
  treated as unique. Some groups (Spanish speakers, people of colour) more often leave feeling unheard.
- The most frustrating steps are booking, check-in and the waiting room; scheduling trouble and waits are top
  reasons to switch. Expectations are low, so beating them stands out.

## Who to win
- **Referring primary care doctors** steer most specialty choices. They want specialists who communicate
  findings, make patients feel heard and coordinate follow-up. Operational coordination is a marketable benefit.
- **Communities** of patients and families with the same condition create loyalty and trust.
- **Staff and recruits:** shortages are shifting budget toward recruitment marketing; build one brand that works for both.

## How to position and spend
- Most systems claim one of five positions: whole person; innovation and research; humanity and kindness;
  access to specialists; top-ranked quality. Find the nuance your local rivals leave open, and decide whether
  you are leaning into current strengths or shifting perceptions.
- Overall rankings persuade less than they used to; specialty rankings still work low in the funnel (search).
- Personal patient stories move emotion; multi-service vignettes show breadth. Pick by the perception you need to change.
- Search is the base channel (it targets by condition and converts); social works best on individual stories and
  away from politically charged topics; physicians and staff are effective, credible voices online.
- Interest in healthcare brands rises in autumn and winter and dips in spring and summer.
- Tie media to business results (appointments, acquisitions, revenue by service line), with consistent tagging,
  even if the data systems are imperfect.
- Trends to plan for: price transparency as patients act like consumers; research on video and social platforms
  before visits; AI-enabled personalisation, communicated with privacy in mind.
