# Continuous improvement: the twelve lessons and a maturity self-check

From a consulting firm's study of about 20 leaders at 15 health systems in 8 countries (published around
2019-2020). Paraphrased; use it to place a provider on the journey and pick the next step.

## Twelve lessons
To launch:
1. Build a whole management system, not a separate improvement team.
2. Senior leaders change their own behaviour first.
3. Go slower to go faster: plan, think and act over several years.
4. Use one shared language about patients, quality and safety.
5. Tailor training to how different staff engage; keep coaching after it.
6. Show early impact, measured and reported to staff and patients.

To sustain:
7. Pick a few priorities and hold them.
8. Support middle managers; they carry it at every level.
9. Tie frontline improvement work to the organisation's "true north" (a few long-term goals).
10. Involve patients in improvement work.
11. Get management and culture right before buying digital tools.
12. Expect no quick fixes; give the organisation time to learn and adapt.

## Maturity self-check (score each row 0 to 3)
| Dimension | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Focus and alignment | Mission and values, no balanced measures; unit goals unclear | Balanced scorecard of 50+ measures (safety, quality, patient, staff, finance) | A few true-north measures; annual deployment links them to frontline units | True north understood at every level; annual breakthrough goals sized to capacity |
| Leadership behaviour | Top team not aligned; command and control | 2-3 leaders drive change; reverts to top-down under pressure | CEO leads; leaders spend 30%+ of time at the front line, use standard work, join huddles | Leaders see coaching as the job, model root-cause problem solving and humble inquiry, have their own development plans |
| Daily management | Varies by unit; reactive firefighting | Common tools and scorecards; visual boards for huddles; little frontline input | Daily improvement routine generates ideas and coaching; weak link to senior team | Daily system connects true north to middle managers and front line; it is "how we work" and part of manager training |
| People development | No structured improvement training | Basic training; a small central team runs big projects | Structured capability building with post-training coaching; Lean, data, project and change skills | Built into HR competencies and role expectations; tailored for board, leaders, managers and physicians |
| Improvement method | Little data-driven problem solving; same problems recur | Some DMAIC or rapid improvement events; hard to resource and sustain | Larger value-stream efforts (e.g. surgical flow) led centrally; front line in every event | End-to-end value streams aligned to true north; patients in most events |

Use: score each row, aim the next 12 months at moving the lowest row up one level, and recheck yearly.
