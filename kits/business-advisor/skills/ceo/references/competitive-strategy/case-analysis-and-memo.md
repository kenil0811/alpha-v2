# Analysing a business situation and writing the memo

The case method, turned into steps for a real business problem, plus a writing
standard. Use for any recommendation memo, not only strategy.

## Analyse
1. **Frame.** Put yourself in the decision-maker's seat. What do they want? What blocks them? What decision
   must they make? What is at stake? Read any brief or question set first; then ask whether the question
   itself is the right one.
2. **Read twice.** Skim the material once for the gist; then read closely for the facts that matter.
3. **Symptoms.** List the evidence of trouble: financial (share, sales), people (conflict, morale), operational
   (errors, delays). Different functions notice different symptoms; check you have not missed a category.
4. **Diagnose root causes.** Separate surface causes from deep ones. "Poor communication" is surface; "no
   structure exists for the two departments to coordinate" is deep, and suggests a fix. Check that the
   central problem and the secondary ones are consistent with each other.
5. **Alternatives.** Generate several realistic options that address the causes; list pros and cons of each.
6. **Recommend.** Choose from the alternatives; the choice should follow from the evaluation. Do not recommend
   anything not on the list, and do not list options unrelated to the causes. State long-run implications
   and how to soften any long-run downside.
7. **Numbers.** State assumptions and why they are reasonable; explain the model in words so a reader need not
   open the spreadsheet; always run sensitivity analysis; check the model is correct (verification) and
   represents the business (validation). Where assumptions are shaky, recommend how to test them.

A careful recommendation from real analysis beats a lucky right answer; real situations rarely have one.

## Write
- **Two tests:** is it correct (concepts applied properly, numbers right) and is it consistent (every part of
  the argument agrees)? If you call a market unattractive and still recommend investing, say exactly why.
- **The boss standard:** the best work can be passed up the line without changes; weak work makes the reader
  redo it. Work that a chatbot could have produced counts as weak.
- **Structure:** audience and purpose first; executive summary with the recommendation and the ask up front;
  answer the reader's decision questions in the order they would ask them; one keyword heading per question;
  headings that together read as the argument.
- **Evidence:** concrete numbers in each section; compare with alternatives, rivals or averages; apply the
  concept to the facts, don't explain the concept.
- **Exhibits:** only if the text says what to see in each one; unreferenced exhibits are ignored. Keep charts
  small, next to the text, with a caption that states the message.
- **Style:** "we/our/you"; actors as subjects and actions as verbs; sentences of 10 to 20 words, paragraphs of
  2 to 6 sentences; parallel lists; one or two fonts; consistent formatting (readers read a change in form as a
  change in meaning); white space.
- **Brevity:** answer only what was asked; don't list every possible answer hoping one is right. Draft, then edit
  for clarity.
