# Funder readiness rubric (how a funder scores an agency)

Rebuilt in plain words from the kind of scoring guide a regional funder uses for safety-net funding applications. Use it to prepare a funding application, or as a health check a board runs on its own organisation before a funder does.

## Scale
Each criterion is scored 0, 2, 5 or 7. The pattern is the same throughout:
- **0**: no evidence.
- **2**: something exists, but partial, or it is unclear whether it is systematic.
- **5**: a systematic process exists, but evidence is limited in scope.
- **7**: a systematic *and* effective approach, with strong evidence.
The jump from 5 to 7 is evidence: documents, data and examples, not descriptions.

## Administration (9 criteria)
1. Mission, vision and values written down and visibly used in daily practice.
2. Clear core competencies, and a strategic plan that actually drives operations.
3. Senior leaders push for high performance and continuous learning.
4. A systematic way to measure, review and improve performance.
5. Client ("voice of the customer") data collected and used to judge whether needs are met.
6. Processes to evaluate, reward, develop and engage staff and volunteers.
7. A systematic approach to diversity, equity and inclusion in the workplace.
8. Processes to document and meet legal, ethical and regulatory requirements.
9. Electronic systems giving real-time administrative, financial and performance data.

## Governance (7 criteria)
1. A governance structure in line with the funder's standards (for example at least 12 directors, standing committees, at least four meetings a year).
2. Processes to recruit, develop, engage and retain directors (all four).
3. A systematic approach to diversity, equity and inclusion on the board.
4. Bylaws, policies and oversight covering transparency, stewardship and accountability to the community.
5. Succession plans for the chief executive, board chair and other key roles.
6. The board assesses itself individually and collectively at least once a year.
7. The board reviews the chief executive annually and uses the review to set pay and goals.

## Program (10 criteria)
1. The program addresses a need named in the community needs assessment, with the link explained.
2. A systematic way to deliver program activities.
3. A defined target population that matches both the need and the program's activities and outcomes.
4. A theory of change (how activities lead to client outcomes), backed by current research, evidence or experience.
5. Successful outcome results for the target population, compared with internal trends and external benchmarks.
6. Processes to keep the program faithful to its model and to adjust it when needed.
7. Processes to listen to participants and turn what they say into action.
8. Evidence that programs reduce disparities, with outcomes, not just intent.
9. Staff and volunteers have the qualifications, training, credentials and cultural competence needed.
10. Real collaboration with other providers (shared resources, joint funding bids, coalitions), not just referrals.

## Finance (10 criteria, several with numeric bands)
| Criterion | Full marks (7) | 5 | 2 | 0 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| How the funds will be used for the stated needs and outcomes | fully described | somewhat | partially | not described |
| Financial expertise for planning, monitoring and safeguarding | sufficient | some | minimal | none |
| Impact of not receiving this funding | strong need shown and impact explained | some reasoning | partial | none |
| Alternative funding sources named | four or more | two or three | one | none |
| Operating reserve ratio | 25-75% | 20-25% or 75-100% | 10-20% or over 100% | under 10% or negative |
| Support services (overhead) ratio | 6-25% | under 6% | 25-35% | over 35% |
| Revenue diversity | four or more types making up over 85% | three or more types making up 40-85% of revenue | one or two types making up 10-40% | no diversity |
| Deficits in the past three years | none | one | two | three |
| Auditor's management letter | no weaknesses | up to three, with an action plan | five or more, some action | severe, no plan |
| Budget figures match the audited financials | match, with reasonable explanations | match, explanations partly clear | match but unexplained | don't match, no explanation |

Note the bands: both too little and too much reserve, and both too high and suspiciously low overhead, lose points.

## How a board uses it
1. Score the organisation honestly, criterion by criterion, with the evidence you would attach.
2. List every criterion below 5; sort by how cheaply it can be fixed (often a written policy, an annual board self-assessment or a CEO review cycle).
3. Assign owners: governance items to the governance committee, finance items to the finance committee, program items to staff with a board liaison.
4. Re-score before the next application cycle.
See also `financial-oversight.md` for the funder's quality standards behind the finance items.
