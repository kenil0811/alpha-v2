# Financial oversight checklist for a nonprofit board

Rebuilt in plain words from a typical funder's quality standards for agencies it funds.
Use it to review an organisation before a budget or audit vote.

## Stability
- Operating reserves: a board-approved target; a common benchmark is unrestricted
  (non-capital) net assets of 25-75 percent of annual expenses (about 3-9 months).
- Working capital or a line of credit to cover cash shortfalls.
- Unrestricted net assets grew in at least two of the last three years, unless a deficit was planned.
- Revenue diverse enough that losing one source does not stop operations.
- A plan for sudden revenue loss or unusual expense.
- A policy capping accrued paid leave.

## Planning and budget
- The board approves a written, program-level budget before the fiscal year starts.
- Management and board both take part; the budget reflects goals, projected funding, the
  reserve target, fixed and variable costs, direct and indirect costs, cost per unit of service.
- The strategic plan includes financial goals; each program's revenue, expenses and trends are
  reviewed in planning; new programs are approved with their financial impact; growth does not
  outrun infrastructure.

## Monitoring
- One or more board members (finance committee) review budget-to-actual statements at least
  quarterly, explain significant variances, watch trends, and recommend budget revisions.
- The full board receives financial reports at least quarterly.
- Management produces monthly budget-to-actual reports, monitors cash flow, receivables and
  payables monthly, and proposes action on variances.
- Investments reviewed quarterly by a board member.

## Audit
- An independent CPA, approved by the board, audits annually; aim for an unqualified (clean) opinion.
- A board member or audit committee interviews and recommends the firm, meets the auditor
  after fieldwork, reviews the draft statements and management letter, makes sure the audit is
  done within six months of year-end, and follows up on the letter's recommendations.
- Figures given to funders match the audited statements.
- Supporting costs (management, general and fundraising) under 25 percent of total revenue.

## Internal controls
- GAAP reporting, accrual basis internally, a written accounting procedures manual.
- Segregation of duties: the person who receives payments is not the one who posts them; bank
  statements go to someone other than the person who reconciles; a second person reviews the
  monthly reconciliation.
- Dual signatures above a threshold; the chief executive's expenses approved by a board member;
  competitive bids for large purchases; journal entries approved; staff with cash access
  bonded or insured; finance staff take at least five consecutive days off a year.
- Ledger updated and reconciled monthly.

## Compliance
- Payroll and other taxes paid and filed on time (IRS Form 990, employment taxes); benefits
  obligations current; state registration current; insurance coverage reviewed periodically.
