# Running a board review

How a board diagnoses itself and decides where to spend its limited time. Synthesised from the McKinsey framework for dynamic nonprofit boards and its self-assessment grid; wording ours. SKILL.md step 9 has the rating scale.

## Three roles a strong board plays
1. **Shape direction**: mission, vision, strategy and key policies, together with staff.
2. **Ensure leadership and resources**: the right chief executive, enough money, and directors' own expertise, access and reputation put to work.
3. **Monitor and improve performance**: of the organisation, the executive and the board itself.
Supported by enablers: size and structure, composition, leadership, and processes (meetings, information, orientation).

## Shift focus as conditions change
A dynamic board covers every role at a minimum standard but puts most of its time where the organisation needs it now. Review these factors at least yearly (some boards do it quarterly):
| Factor | What it means for board focus |
|---|---|
| **Life stage** | Young, founder-led: hands-on help with operations and execution. Maturing: formal processes, more challenge to the executive, founders allowed to "term out", recruit a couple of new directors at a time. |
| **Strength of chief executive and staff** | Weak: the board fills gaps and may lead a search. Strong: the board adds expertise and access instead, and guards against complacency. |
| **Stability of income** | Unstable or over-concentrated funding: more board time on fundraising. Stable: more on strategy and performance. |
| **Change in the social issue** | The need grows, shrinks or shifts: revisit mission and programs. |
| **Competitive or funding landscape** | Stronger peers or donor shifts: question the strategy, consider partnerships. |
Don't let the crisis of the moment or one director's passion set the board's agenda.

## Four-step board review
1. **Set up a formal process**: a small committee (often chair plus chief executive, sometimes an outside facilitator for anonymity), a timetable and agreed outputs.
2. **Diagnose**: survey directors and senior staff on each responsibility (performance 1-4, importance 1-3); add open comments.
3. **Share and discuss**: circulate results to the whole board; discuss gaps between board and staff views and areas of disagreement; reach a shared view.
4. **Implement**: decide whether fixes need a committee, a policy, recruitment or retraining; name owners and dates. Expect years, not months, for a full redesign.

## Prioritise with performance vs importance
Plot each responsibility by average performance (poor to distinctive) and importance (low to high):
| | Low performance | High performance |
|---|---|---|
| **High importance** | Step change: major board time and energy | Reinforce and keep investing |
| **Low importance** | Incremental change if resources allow | Monitor; maybe redirect effort elsewhere |
Pick two or three items in the top-left box for the coming year.
