# Getting better at negotiating: practice, debrief, cheat sheet

Negotiation skill grows with repetition and honest review.

## 1. Practice on low-stakes, real asks
Before a big negotiation season, start at least two real negotiations you would normally skip: ask a supplier for a better price or payment terms, ask for an earlier delivery slot, ask "what is your discounted price?" A "no" is fine; the point is comfort with asking. Rules: real stakes, courtesy, no false promises.

After each one, write a short log:
- How did I prepare?
- How hard was it, and what surprised me?
- How good was the outcome against my reservation point?
- Where was I more or less effective?
- What will I do differently next time?

## 2. Debrief every significant negotiation
Within a day, answer:
1. What did I do well?
2. What would I do differently, concretely?
3. How did the outcome compare with my reservation point, target and the likely zone of agreement?
4. How did my view change once I learned more about the other side's real position (their alternatives, priorities, constraints)? What did I misread during the talks?
5. Which concept explains what happened (anchoring, logrolling, BATNA strength, subjective value)?
If the relationship allows, ask the counterpart afterwards what mattered to them and what they would have traded; their answers are the best data you will get.

## 3. Peer coaching
Once or twice a year, spend 90 minutes with two or three colleagues who have seen you negotiate (ideally including someone who sat across from you). Each person brings strengths, weaknesses and one recurring pattern; others add what they observed and one suggestion. Go beyond war stories to patterns.

## 4. Keep a one-page cheat sheet
Write your own one-page list of the points you most need reminding of, and reread it before important talks. Start from the prep sheet (`prep-sheet.md`) and add the lessons your debriefs keep surfacing (for example "I concede too fast after the first counter" or "I forget to ask why").
