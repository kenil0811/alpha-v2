# Running effective board meetings

> **Area:** `board-meetings` · owned by the CEO skill. **Use when:** Plan and run an effective board or committee meeting: packet timing, an inform-discuss-decide agenda, Robert's Rules motions and votes, and minutes that stand as a legal record. Use when preparing a board agenda, chairing or attending a formal meeting, drafting a motion, or writing minutes.

**Outcome:** board meetings that spend their time on the future, make clear, lawful decisions,
and leave a clean record.

## When to use
- Drafting an agenda or board packet.
- Chairing a meeting, or speaking in one as a member.
- Wording a motion, amending one, or deciding how to vote.
- Writing or approving minutes.

## Method

### 1. Purpose of the meeting
A board meeting exists for fiduciary oversight (is the organisation sound?), strategic thinking
(1-3 years out), and policy (rules of the road for the chief executive). It is not for operations.

### 2. Before the meeting
- Chair and executive agree the agenda; every item is labelled **Inform**, **Discuss** or **Decide**.
- Send the packet 5-7 days ahead; longer for big decisions (bylaw changes, strategic plan approval).
- Members read the packet before they arrive. A new member who cannot read the financials
  asks the treasurer for a short one-to-one beforehand.
- Use a consent agenda (standard practice) to approve routine items in
  one vote: prior minutes, routine reports.
- Aim for about 20 percent of time on reports of the past and 80 percent on the future.

### 3. Standard order of business
1. Call to order.
2. Roll call; confirm a **quorum** (the minimum present to act, set in the bylaws).
3. Approve the previous minutes.
4. Officer reports: chair, treasurer (budget, statements, audit status); vote on major commitments.
5. Committee reports (action items only).
6. Special orders and unfinished business (items carried over).
7. New business: proposals, motions, strategic discussion.
8. Public comment if the bylaws require it.
9. Executive session if needed (personnel, legal, real estate), without staff or guests.
10. Announcements; adjournment.

### 4. Motions in six steps
1. A member gets recognised by the chair and says "I move that ..." (always phrased positively).
2. Another member says "Second" (it means "worth discussing", not agreement). No second, no motion.
3. The chair restates it: "It is moved and seconded that ..." It now belongs to the meeting;
   the mover cannot change it without consent.
4. Debate: the mover speaks first; remarks go to the chair; no one speaks twice until all who
   wish have spoken once; no personal attacks.
5. Vote: the chair restates the motion word for word and calls for ayes and noes.
6. Announce: "The ayes have it, the motion is adopted" (or lost). Record it in the minutes.

### 5. Motions to know
| To do this | Say | Debatable | Vote |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bring business | "I move that ..." | Yes | Majority |
| Change wording | "I move to amend by ..." | Yes | Majority |
| Send to committee | "I move to refer to ..." | Yes | Majority |
| Delay to a set time | "I move to postpone until ..." | Yes | Majority |
| Set aside for now | "I move to lay on the table" | No | Majority |
| Limit debate | "I move to limit debate to ..." | No | Two-thirds |
| End debate, vote now | "I move the previous question" | No | Two-thirds |
| Break | "I move to recess for ..." | No | Majority |
| End meeting | "I move to adjourn" | No | Majority |

Points that need no motion: **point of order** (rules broken or off agenda; raise at once),
**point of information** (need a fact before voting), **parliamentary inquiry** (which motion
achieves what I want), **point of privilege** (cannot hear, room too hot).
Other tools: divide the question (split a motion into parts that stand alone), consider by
paragraph (for long documents), general consent ("if there is no objection ...") for routine items.

### 6. Voting methods
Voice; division (show of hands or standing to check a voice vote); roll call (when each vote
must be on record); general consent (silence means yes; one objection forces a vote); ballot
(when secrecy matters). Bylaws decide which applies.

### 7. Minutes
- A legal record of what was done, not what was said.
- Include: organisation, date, time, place; who presided; members present and absent; quorum;
  approval of prior minutes; each motion as worded, mover (seconder optional), and result;
  reports received; time of adjournment; secretary's signature.
- Distribute promptly; approve at the next meeting; retain permanently.

### 8. Conduct as a member
- Listen for the first two meetings; then ask "why do we do it this way?".
- Disagree inside the room; once a vote passes, support it outside ("one voice").
- Disclose conflicts and step out of votes where you have one.
- Formal procedure suits fiduciary and strategic decisions; for open-ended framing questions,
  switch to a facilitated discussion without motions, then return to procedure to decide
  (see `nonprofit-board-governance`, generative mode).

## Worked pattern
Agenda item: "Approve FY27 budget (Decide)". Packet sent 7 days ahead with budget, variance
notes and reserve projection. Treasurer presents in 5 minutes. A member moves "that the board
approve the FY27 operating budget of $2.4M as presented"; seconded; chair restates. A member
moves to amend by adding "with a mid-year review in January"; amendment passes by voice;
the amended motion passes 11-1 by roll call because a member asked for the record. Minutes
record both motions, results and the roll call.

## Common mistakes
- Packets sent the night before; members reading during the meeting.
- Whole meetings of staff reports with no decision.
- Debating before a motion is stated; motions phrased negatively.
- Minutes that transcribe discussion instead of recording actions.
- Using "table" to kill a motion without the meeting realising it can be taken back up.
- Undue strictness that silences members; the rules exist to help deliberation.
