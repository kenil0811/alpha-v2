# Chief executive: strategy, competition and governance

**Outcome:** one owner for every business method this role needs. Each area below is a full method (outcome, when to use, method, worked pattern, common mistakes, sources, gaps) kept in its own reference file.

## How to use

1. Find the area or areas that match the task with "Which area when", then confirm in the table below.
2. Open each matching file and follow its method. Paths are relative to the module's `skills/` folder.
3. A name in backticks that is not in this table belongs to another role; the list at the end says which, and its file is `<role>/references/<name>.md`. Work that spans roles starts from `chief-of-staff/SKILL.md`.
4. Say which area the method came from.

## Which area when

**The usual questions**

- "What is our strategy? Why are we more or less profitable than rivals? Can this be copied?" → `competitive-strategy`
- "Is this market worth entering or investing in? Why is it so hard to make money here?" → `industry-analysis`
- "If we cut price or add capacity, what will they do?" → `competitive-moves`
- "Should we get into X, make it or buy it, acquire or partner, license or build?" → `corporate-strategy`
- "What does AI, a marketplace or a digital rival do to us? Products to solutions?" → `platform-and-technology-strategy`
- "Why do we keep getting the worst customers, hires or vendors? How should we pay for performance?" → `incentives-and-information`
- "Why is our practice or clinic losing money? Which payors pay?" → `healthcare-business-economics`
- "Prepare the board agenda, a motion or the minutes" → `board-meetings`
- "Is our nonprofit board doing its job? Is this the board's call or management's?" → `nonprofit-board-governance`
- "Scope and run a project for a client" → `consulting-engagement`
- "I am about to negotiate price or terms with one counterpart" → `negotiation-deal-making`
- "Three or more parties, a broker in the middle, a live dispute or another culture" → `complex-negotiations-and-disputes`
- "Write the business plan, executive summary or pitch deck; settle legal form, founders' agreement and IP" → `business-plan-and-pitch`

**Look-alikes, told apart**

- `industry-analysis` asks whether the market is worth being in; `competitive-strategy` how one business wins in it; `competitive-moves` how rivals answer one specific move; `corporate-strategy` which businesses to be in and how to hold them; `platform-and-technology-strategy` how a technology shifts value along the chain.
- `corporate-strategy` make-buy-ally is the strategic test (capabilities, control, VRIO); the CFO's `relevant-cost-decisions` is the cost test. Use both for a real make-or-buy.
- `negotiation-deal-making` is one counterpart, prepared and bargained; `complex-negotiations-and-disputes` is many parties, agents, constituents, disputes, cultures or a stuck deal. A supply contract's design (buy-back, revenue sharing) is the COO's `supply-contracts-and-coordination`.
- `board-meetings` is how to run the meeting (agenda, motions, minutes); `nonprofit-board-governance` is what the board is for and whether it is healthy.
- `business-plan-and-pitch` is the venture's documents; the CMO's `business-storytelling` shapes any deck that must win a decision; the CMO's `strategic-communication-plan` is one story for all stakeholders.
- `incentives-and-information` designs pay and contracts against hidden information; the CHRO's `leading-and-motivating-people` re-engages people; the CHRO's `people-analytics` checks the measures pay rests on.

**Often used together**

- Strategy review: `industry-analysis` → `competitive-strategy` → `competitive-moves` → the CMO's `business-storytelling`.
- Grow by acquisition or alliance: `corporate-strategy` → the CFO's `valuation-multiples-and-deals` → `negotiation-deal-making` → the CHRO's `leading-change` for integration.
- Start a venture: the CMO's `opportunity-validation` → `market-sizing-and-unit-economics` → `business-plan-and-pitch` → the CFO's `raising-startup-capital`.
- Client project: `consulting-engagement` → the areas the problem needs → the CMO's `business-storytelling`.

## Areas

| Area | Use when | File |
|---|---|---|
| `competitive-strategy` | Write down, test and sharpen how a business wins - who it serves, what it offers, the practices that deliver it, and why rivals cannot copy it; before a big, hard-to-reverse choice. | `ceo/references/competitive-strategy.md` |
| `industry-analysis` | Explain who captures the profit in a market and whether it is worth entering, expanding in, or investing in, using the five forces, substitutes from outside change, and the industry cost (supply) curve. | `ceo/references/industry-analysis.md` |
| `platform-and-technology-strategy` | Work out how a new technology or platform shifts value and bargaining power along a value chain, and choose a response - build, partner, acquire (including a partner whose power has grown), escape supplier-set margins, earn money from a technology given away, turn a community into commerce, run a marketplace where infrastructure is thin, move from products to solution selling, plan a product roadmap, commercialise a technology that is not ready yet, or govern AI adoption inside the firm. | `ceo/references/platform-and-technology-strategy.md` |
| `competitive-moves` | Anticipate how rivals will react before a price, capacity or entry move, using game theory (payoff matrices, game trees, credible commitments, vertical and preemptive moves as commitments, entering against entrenched incumbents) and the standard oligopoly models. | `ceo/references/competitive-moves.md` |
| `corporate-strategy` | Decide which businesses to be in and how to hold them - diversify (related or unrelated), make, buy or ally, acquire, license or run an internal venture, review the portfolio and fit the structure - using VRIO, the vertical-integration diamond, synergy tests, a markets-first acquisition playbook, culture end states for integration and staged NPV trees. | `ceo/references/corporate-strategy.md` |
| `incentives-and-information` | Fix problems from one side knowing more than the other - adverse selection, moral hazard, managers pursuing their own goals - with screening, signals, monitoring and pay-for-performance; for pay plans, warranties, supplier contracts and offers that attract the wrong buyers. | `ceo/references/incentives-and-information.md` |
| `healthcare-business-economics` | Run the money side of a healthcare provider: market structure and care pathway, payors and payer mix, revenue-cycle leakage, supply costs, social-needs programmes and clinical IT, fraud and abuse rules, and strategy under scenarios on a time cone. | `ceo/references/healthcare-business-economics.md` |
| `board-meetings` | Plan and run an effective board or committee meeting: packet timing, an inform-discuss-decide agenda, Robert's Rules motions and votes, and minutes that stand as a legal record. | `ceo/references/board-meetings.md` |
| `nonprofit-board-governance` | Serve on, assess or strengthen a nonprofit board: directors' legal duties, the board's ten responsibilities, the board-chief executive split, committees, financial oversight, the three modes of governing, and a self-assessment. | `ceo/references/nonprofit-board-governance.md` |
| `consulting-engagement` | Run a client project end to end: kick-off and probing questions, problem definition, a statement of work, a research plan built on what must be true, a mid-point check-in, and a final deliverable that ends in a decision. | `ceo/references/consulting-engagement.md` |
| `negotiation-deal-making` | Prepare and run a negotiation with one counterpart (supplier, customer, lease, hire, salary, partnership): BATNA, reservation, target, ZOPA, offers and counters, trades across issues, relationship and ethics. | `ceo/references/negotiation-deal-making.md` |
| `complex-negotiations-and-disputes` | Handle negotiations beyond one-on-one deals: disputes and claims, multi-party deals and coalitions, labour-contract renewals, agents and constituents, power plays, cross-cultural counterparts, and why a deal is stuck. | `ceo/references/complex-negotiations-and-disputes.md` |
| `business-plan-and-pitch` | Write what a young venture uses to win money, partners or a competition - the written business plan, the one-page executive summary, the pitch deck, the elevator pitch and the ask - and settle the founder decisions the plan rests on: legal form, founders' agreement, IP and the harvest. | `ceo/references/business-plan-and-pitch.md` |
